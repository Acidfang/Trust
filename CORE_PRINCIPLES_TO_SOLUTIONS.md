## PRINCIPLE 1: MULTI-CONSTRAINT MANAGEMENT
**What it solves**: Why systems need multiple simultaneous conditions. Why you can't just "remove one thing"

**Solutions**:
- **Medical diagnosis**: Symptom only reveals if you map ALL constraints involved
  - Fatigue could be thyroid (constraint 1), vitamin B12 (constraint 2), sleep apnea (constraint 3)
  - Treating one without identifying others = treatment failure
  
- **System reliability engineering**: Don't eliminate single failure points, identify ALL constraints that must hold
  - Bridge fails if: materials fail OR joints fail OR loads unexpected OR design wrong
  - Safety comes from checking ALL four simultaneously, not perfectly perfecting one
  
- **Organizational dysfunction**: Problems aren't single root cause
  - Productivity low = could be morale (constraint) AND clarity (constraint) AND tools (constraint)
  - Fix morale alone = still stuck
  - Map the dependency web first

- **Drug interactions**: Multiple simultaneous constraints
  - Drug A constrains liver metabolism, Drug B uses that same pathway
  - Can't predict interaction without mapping BOTH constraints

**Decision trigger**: "Why didn't fixing X solve the problem?" → Look for other constraints

## PRINCIPLE 2: CONSTRAINT FAILURE CASCADES
**What it solves**: Why things suddenly catastrophically fail. Why prevention is exponentially better than response.

**Solutions**:
- **Detonation prevention**: 
  - Fuel alone = safe. Ignition alone = safe. But together = explosive cascade
  - Separate fuel from ignition source = cascade prevention
  - Applicable to all cascade systems
  
- **Organizational collapse prevention**:
  - One person leaving = fine. Two key people leaving = can survive. Three = cascade begins
  - Solution: Identify cascade-vulnerable nodes (people/systems/roles where N failures trigger (N+1)th failure)
  - Redundancy ONLY at cascade-trigger points, not everywhere
  
- **Immune collapse (AIDS)**:
  - CD4 count drops below N → cascade begins
  - Solution: Before cascade threshold, interventions are optional. After, mandatory and less effective
  - Preventive intervention target = threshold, not magnitude
  
- **Market crashes**:
  - First seller OK. Second seller OK. At N sellers → cascade to crash
  - Solution: Identify the cascade-trigger number
  - Circuit breakers work because they interrupt cascade paths
  
- **Cancer progression**:
  - First mutation = cell has safeguards. Second = safeguards compromised. Third = cascade
  - Solution: Test for cascade-vulnerability (which 2 mutations make the 3rd catastrophic?)

**Decision trigger**: "What's the failure threshold?" → Look for cascade architecture

## PRINCIPLE 3: OPTIMAL CONSTRAINT CALIBRATION
**What it solves**: Why "more" or "less" both fail. Why systems have sweet spots.

**Solutions**:
- **Learning optimization**: 
  - Challenge too easy = boredom = no learning. Challenge too hard = frustration = no learning
  - Solution: Dynamically adjust challenge to maintain equidistance from both boundaries
  - Applicable to training programs, game design, education
  
- **Organizational hierarchy**:
  - Too centralized = slow, brittle. Too decentralized = chaotic
  - Solution: Measure decision latency + variance in outcomes. Sweet spot minimizes both
  - Can calculate optimal hierarchy depth for each organization size
  
- **Immune tolerance**:
  - Tolerance too strict = attacks self. Tolerance too loose = misses pathogens
  - Solution: Co-stimulation requirement calibrates middle ground
  - Can measure tolerance threshold
  
- **Temperature in reactions**:
  - Too cold = no reaction. Too hot = decomposition
  - Solution: Activation energy identifies the sweet spot
  - Applicable to ALL rate-controlled processes
  
- **Social tolerance for diversity**:
  - Too homogeneous = fragile, brittle, misses information. Too diverse = chaos
  - Solution: Map to constraint-uncertainty tradeoff
  - Optimal = max diversity that maintains coherence
  
- **Stress management**:
  - No stress = atrophy. Chronic stress = damage. Intermittent stress = adaptation
  - Solution: Measure recovery ratio (work:rest ratio)
  - Different domains have different optimal ratios (muscles vs. immune vs. cognition)

**Decision trigger**: "We tried more, didn't help. We tried less, didn't help either." → Solve for sweet spot, not direction

## PRINCIPLE 4: CONSTRAINT AS INFORMATION/FEEDBACK
**What it solves**: Why removing all constraints makes systems blind. Why constraints carry data.

**Solutions**:
- **Price signals in economics**:
  - Price constrains purchase. But constraint IS information about scarcity
  - Solution: Can't solve shortage by removing price. Must understand WHAT the constraint is signaling
  - Price spike tells you "information cascade happened" or "supply actually dropped"
  - Different constraint sources require different responses
  
- **Pain as diagnostic**:
  - Pain constrains activity but SIGNALS the constraint type
  - Sharp vs. dull vs. throbbing = different constraints (nerve, inflammation, pressure)
  - Solution: Don't eliminate pain, read it first
  
- **Boredom as signal**:
  - Boredom constrains focus but signals "no novelty in environment"
  - Solution: Boredom isn't failure to suppress, it's data that environment needs change
  - Used correctly = adaptive exploration trigger
  
- **Anxiety as signal**:
  - Anxiety constrains action but signals "mismatch between expectation and reality"
  - What mismatch? Before eliminating anxiety, identify what it's signaling
  - Anxiety without source = just noise. Anxiety with identified source = diagnostic
  
- **Error messages as constraints**:
  - Error constrains system but carries information
  - "Suppressing error" = losing diagnosis
  - Solution: Every constraint should be readable (good error messages, clear feedback loops)
  
- **Negative feedback loops in systems**:
  - Feedback is a constraint that carries information about deviation from setpoint
  - Solution: Systems without feedback are blind
  - Can diagnose system health by reading its feedback patterns

**Decision trigger**: "What is this constraint TELLING us?" → Read the signal before removing the constraint

## PRINCIPLE 5: NONLINEAR/THRESHOLD RESPONSE
**What it solves**: Why small changes sometimes cause huge shifts. Why predictions fail at edges.

**Solutions**:
- **Phase transitions**:
  - Temperature continuous but state discontinuous
  - Solution: Identify the threshold. Below = one behavior. Above = completely different
  - Applied: Boiling point, freezing point, critical temperature for superconductivity
  - Can calculate threshold from underlying parameters
  
- **Anxiety manifestation**:
  - Mild stress = adaptive. Moderate stress = still OK. Past threshold = panic cascade
  - Solution: Identify YOUR threshold (varies per person)
  - Intervention BEFORE threshold = simple. After threshold = requires much more
  
- **Cardiac arrhythmia**:
  - Heart rate slightly off = compensated. Slightly more off = still compensated. Threshold = sudden failure
  - Solution: Cardiac monitoring looks for threshold-approach (increasing variability precedes sudden collapse)
  - Intervention at threshold-approach saves life. After crossing = too late
  
- **Neural firing**:
  - Stimulus below threshold = no firing. Above = full firing
  - Solution: Synaptic plasticity works by adjusting thresholds, not magnitudes
  - This explains learning at threshold level
  
- **Stock market crashes**:
  - Gradual losses OK. Past threshold = cascade selling begins
  - Solution: Circuit breakers placed at known thresholds
  - Same principle used in avalanche prevention (trigger points)
  
- **Organizational burnout**:
  - Gradual increase in workload compensated. Past threshold = sudden collapse
  - Solution: Monitor threshold-approach indicators (sick days, error rates, turnover precursors)
  - Intervention at threshold-approach prevents collapse

**Decision trigger**: "This changed suddenly with a small input change" → Look for nonlinear response. Find the threshold.

## PRINCIPLE 6: CONSTRAINT REMOVAL AS EXPLORATION
**What it solves**: Why learning requires exploration. Why controlled risk-taking works.

**Solutions**:
- **REM sleep mechanism**:
  - During REM: sensory input constraints removed
  - Brain can explore scenario-space without environmental consequences
  - Solution: Sleep IS information exploration. Can't learn without it
  - Applicable: Meditation, simulation, imagination use same mechanism
  
- **Evolutionary innovation**:
  - Mutation = constraint relaxation on genetic sequence
  - Some mutations lethal (poor explorations). Most neutral. Rare ones adaptive
  - Solution: Mutation rate is tunable. Too high = mostly lethal. Too low = no exploration
  - Can calculate optimal mutation rate for given environment
  
- **R&D budgets**:
  - R&D = constraining immediate profit to explore future-space
  - Solution: Optimal R&D budget depends on environment change rate
  - Stable market = lower optimal R&D. Volatile market = higher optimal R&D
  
- **Play in animals**:
  - Play = exploration with reduced real-world constraints (rough play, mock fights)
  - Solution: Play time prepares for future scenarios
  - Can measure how much play is needed for behavioral adaptation
  
- **Controlled experiments**:
  - Experiment = temporarily removing constraints to explore causal relationships
  - Solution: Experiment design determines what constraints to remove
  - Sloppy experiment = remove too many constraints = can't identify causal chain
  
- **Therapy (psychological)**:
  - Therapy = safe exploration space where normal constraints temporarily lifted
  - Solution: Therapeutic container = removal of judgment/consequence constraints
  - Can't process trauma under normal constraints. Must remove them temporarily

**Decision trigger**: "How do we explore this space safely?" → Identify constraints to remove, keep others for safety

## PRINCIPLE 7: CONSTRAINT DISTRIBUTION TRADE-OFFS
**What it solves**: Why organizational structures have tradeoffs. Why one-size doesn't fit all.

**Solutions**:
- **Centralized vs. distributed systems**:
  - Centralized: Fast decision, single point of failure, brittle
  - Distributed: Slower decision, robust, adaptive
  - Solution: Different constraints = different optimal distributions
  - Emergency (fire in building) = centralized (follow authority). Long-term (climate) = distributed (everyone adapts)
  
- **Supply chain design**:
  - Centralized warehousing: Efficient, fragile to disruption
  - Distributed inventory: Less efficient, robust
  - Solution: Constraint is "expected disruption frequency"
  - High disruption = more distribution. Low disruption = more centralization
  
- **Attention allocation**:
  - Focus (narrow) = deep processing, misses periphery
  - Diffuse = broad awareness, shallow processing
  - Solution: Task determines distribution. Detail work = focus. Vigilance = diffuse
  - Can't solve vigilance by trying harder to focus. Must distribute attention
  
- **Knowledge storage**:
  - Centralized knowledge base: Efficient retrieval, single failure point
  - Distributed knowledge: Redundant, harder to retrieve
  - Solution: Critical knowledge = must be distributed. Routine knowledge = can centralize
  
- **Governance models**:
  - Monarchy = centralized, fast, brittle
  - Democracy = distributed, slow, robust
  - Solution: Constraint is "rate of environmental change"
  - Slow-change environment = can use monarchy. Fast-change = need democracy

**Decision trigger**: "This system is too slow" or "too fragile" → It's a constraint distribution problem. Adjust the tradeoff.

## PRINCIPLE 8: CONSTRAINT COUPLING (Interdependence)
**What it solves**: Why changing one thing requires adjusting others. Why optimization is hard.

**Solutions**:
- **Drug interactions**:
  - Drug A affects liver (constraint 1). Drug B uses liver
  - Solution: Map constraint coupling graph
  - Any change requires re-checking coupled constraints
  
- **Climate interventions**:
  - Carbon tax couples to energy prices which couples to transportation which couples to development patterns
  - Solution: Can't change one without predicting downstream coupling effects
  - Identify strongest couplings first (highest leverage points)
  
- **Organizational change**:
  - Changing compensation structure (constraint) couples to hiring practices (constraint) couples to performance expectations (constraint)
  - Solution: Change agents must map full coupling graph
  - Single-point changes often fail because coupled constraints still constrain
  
- **Thermodynamic systems**:
  - Pressure couples to temperature couples to volume
  - Le Chatelier's principle = system restores equilibrium when one constraint changes
  - Solution: Can't change one variable independently. Must account for coupling
  
- **Neural networks**:
  - Change weight (constraint) affects all downstream activations
  - Solution: Learning works because it manages constraint coupling
  - Backpropagation = algorithm that respects coupling structure

**Decision trigger**: "We fixed X but Y still broken" → X and Y are coupled. Can't fix independently.

## PRINCIPLE 9: SCALE-DEPENDENT CONSTRAINTS
**What it solves**: Why solutions that work at one scale fail at another.

**Solutions**:
- **Building design**:
  - Ant colony: Distributed decision making works fine at scale 1000
  - Human organization: At scale 1000 needs hierarchy
  - Solution: Leadership structure must scale
  - Can predict required structure from population size
  
- **Physics laws**:
  - Quantum dominates at small scale. Relativity at large scale
  - Classical mechanics in middle
  - Solution: Choose model appropriate to scale
  
- **Evolution**:
  - At small population scale: drift dominates. At large scale: selection dominates
  - Solution: Conservation strategy depends on population size
  - Small population = manage drift. Large population = manage selection
  
- **Communication**:
  - Small group: can use informal consensus. Large group: needs formal voting
  - Solution: Governance structure must scale with group size
  
- **Disease control**:
  - Individual level: treatment. Population level: prevention. Species level: eradication
  - Solution: Same disease, different strategies at different scales

**Decision trigger**: "This worked before but doesn't now" → Scale changed. Constraints change with scale.

## PRINCIPLE 10: CONSTRAINT PRECISION VS COST
**What it solves**: Why you can't always be precise. Why approximations work.

**Solutions**:
- **Engineering tolerances**:
  - Tight tolerance = high precision, high cost
  - Loose tolerance = low precision, low cost
  - Solution: Design to minimum necessary precision
  - Over-specification = wasted cost. Under-specification = failures
  
- **Language learning**:
  - Native speakers use ~20,000 words. Fluency requires ~2,000. Communication needs ~300.
  - Solution: Precision level determines vocabulary needed
  - Can't speak fluently with only 300 words, but can communicate
  
- **Medical screening**:
  - Precise screening = catches more true positives but more false positives too
  - Solution: Choose precision based on downstream costs (treatment cost, harm of false positive)
  - Cancer screening needs different precision than cholesterol screening
  
- **Software specifications**:
  - Precise specs = expensive, delays. Vague specs = cheaper, faster, rework needed
  - Solution: Precision-cost curve determines appropriate spec level
  
- **Measuring physical constants**:
  - Higher precision = exponentially higher cost
  - Solution: Only precision needed for application
  - Speed of light: 6 decimal places for most engineering, more for fundamental physics

**Decision trigger**: "This is too expensive" or "too imprecise" → Adjust the precision-cost tradeoff

## PRINCIPLE 11: CONSTRAINT EFFICIENCY TRADE
**What it solves**: Why habits, protocols, and rituals actually improve performance.

**Solutions**:
- **Habit formation**:
  - Constraint: "You will do this same way every time"
  - Benefit: Frees conscious mind for novel problems
  - Solution: Build habits for routine, preserve conscious capacity for novel
  - Can identify which activities should be habitualized (high-frequency, low-variation)
  
- **Protocols in medical care**:
  - Constraint: "Follow protocol exactly"
  - Benefit: Reduces decision-load per patient, catches edge cases
  - Solution: Protocols work because they trade flexibility for efficiency
  - Can design protocols by identifying decision points that matter most
  
- **Rituals in organizations**:
  - Constraint: "We do this every Tuesday"
  - Benefit: Ensures consistency, signals what's important
  - Solution: Rituals are efficient constraint encoders
  - Can replace 100 memos with one ritual
  
- **Grammar in language**:
  - Constraint: "Follow grammatical rules"
  - Benefit: Enables understanding despite ambiguity
  - Solution: Grammar is a compression algorithm
  - Violate grammar = must add explanation

**Decision trigger**: "People keep making mistakes" → Build a constraint (habit/protocol/rule) to encode correct behavior

## PRINCIPLE 12: CONSTRAINT AS METASTABLE STATE
**What it solves**: Why stable-looking systems suddenly catastrophically fail. Why fragility is hidden.

**Solutions**:
- **Ecosystem stability**:
  - Ecosystem can look stable for decades then crash suddenly
  - Solution: Monitor stability indicators (species diversity, trophic stability, connectivity)
  - Crash happens when you cross threshold from metastable to unstable
  
- **Market bubbles**:
  - Market can look stable (prices rising) but be metastable (detached from fundamentals)
  - Solution: Look for deviation of price from intrinsic value
  - When deviation reaches threshold = bubble will pop
  
- **Autoimmune disease onset**:
  - Person can be immune-stable for years, then suddenly autoimmune
  - Solution: Look for increasing tolerance-breakdown signals
  - Mild food reactions, mild joint pain = threshold-approach indicators
  - Intervention at approach = can prevent crossing threshold
  
- **False vacuum (physics)**:
  - Universe appears stable in current state but is in false vacuum
  - Tiny perturbation = decay to true vacuum
  - Solution: Can only identify by looking for perturbation sources
  - Can't predict if perturbation rare or common

**Decision trigger**: "This seemed stable then fell apart" → It was metastable. Look for hidden perturbation sources.

## PRINCIPLE 13: MEASUREMENT/OBSERVATION AS CONSTRAINT
**What it solves**: Why observation changes outcomes. Why some measurements are invasive.

**Solutions**:
- **Quantum measurement problem**:
  - Measuring position constrains position (collapses superposition)
  - Solution: Can't measure without constraining
  - Choose measurement type based on what you need to know
  - Trade-off: Can't know both position AND momentum precisely
  
- **Surveillance effects**:
  - Being watched constrains behavior (Hawthorne effect)
  - Solution: For studying natural behavior, measurement must be non-invasive
  - If studying performance, measurement changes performance (sometimes for better, sometimes worse)
  
- **Thermometer measurement**:
  - Thermometer absorbs heat, cools the system
  - Solution: Measurement error is real, not a limitation
  - Choose thermometer sensitivity based on acceptable error
  
- **Psychological assessment**:
  - Asking "are you depressed?" influences depression reporting
  - Solution: Multiple measurement types needed (self-report, behavioral, physiological)
  - No single measurement captures full picture because each constrains differently

**Decision trigger**: "The measurement changed the outcome" → That's not a measurement error. That's the physics of observation.

## PRINCIPLE 14: TEMPORAL CONSTRAINT POSITIONING
**What it solves**: Why preparation matters. Why pre-positioning works.

**Solutions**:
- **Immune memory**:
  - First exposure: slow response, ineffective
  - After exposure: memory cells pre-positioned, fast response
  - Solution: Vaccination = pre-positioning without danger
  - Can calculate optimal vaccination timing based on protection duration
  
- **Muscle training**:
  - Training = pre-positioning muscular and neural constraints
  - Solution: Training effect is constraint pre-positioning, not strength gain
  - Can fade quickly if not maintained (constraints degrade over time)
  
- **Emergency preparedness**:
  - Evacuation routes pre-positioned = fast response
  - Solution: Preparation time inversely related to response time when crisis hits
  - Every hour of preparation = saves hours of crisis response
  
- **Infrastructure planning**:
  - Building roads before you need them = constraints pre-positioned for growth
  - Building after growth = catch-up game you can't win
  - Solution: Plan infrastructure based on growth rate forecast
  
- **Learning and transfer**:
  - Early learning = pre-positioning neural constraints for future learning
  - Solution: Can't catch up on foundational learning later
  - Math prerequisites = pre-positioning for advanced math

**Decision trigger**: "We should have prepared earlier" → Temporal positioning problem. Can't fully recover lost prep time.

## PRINCIPLE 15: CONSTRAINT TYPE DETERMINES OUTCOME
**What it solves**: Why the same problem with different root causes needs different solutions.

**Solutions**:
- **Hallucination types**:
  - Visual hallucinations = visual cortex constraint removed
  - Auditory hallucinations = auditory cortex constraint removed
  - Olfactory hallucinations = olfactory cortex constraint removed
  - Solution: Treatment depends on constraint type, not just "has hallucinations"
  
- **Immune diseases**:
  - Tolerance-breakdown (autoimmune) ≠ Response-failure (immunodeficiency)
  - Solution: Same symptom (infection), different constraint failed, different treatment
  - Suppressing immune makes immunodeficiency worse, makes autoimmunity better
  
- **Learning problems**:
  - Attention constraint = ADHD medication helps
  - Processing-speed constraint = ADHD medication doesn't help
  - Memory constraint = different intervention
  - Solution: Same "learning problem", different constraint, different solution
  
- **Market failure**:
  - Information constraint = education/transparency fixes
  - Coordination constraint = rules/enforcement fixes
  - Monopoly constraint = anti-trust fixes
  - Solution: Can't solve information failure with anti-trust. Must match solution to constraint
  
- **Business performance issues**:
  - Quality constraint = process improvement
  - Speed constraint = automation
  - Cost constraint = efficiency
  - Solution: Performance metrics identify which constraint is active

**Decision trigger**: "Our standard solution isn't working" → It's the wrong constraint. Identify the actual constraint type.

## PRINCIPLE 16: CONSTRAINT STACKING/REDUNDANCY
**What it solves**: Why single safeguards fail. Why redundancy works.

**Solutions**:
- **Immune tolerance**:
  - Central tolerance alone: pathogenic memory escapes, autoimmunity occurs
  - Peripheral tolerance alone: strong autoreactive cells escape, autoimmunity occurs
  - Both together: redundancy prevents both failure modes
  - Solution: Identify failure modes first, then add constraints that prevent each
  
- **Safety engineering**:
  - Single seatbelts: Some impacts still fatal
  - Seatbelts + airbags: More failure modes covered
  - Seatbelts + airbags + crumple zones: Even more
  - Solution: Layer constraints based on failure modes
  
- **Financial safeguards**:
  - Single audit: Some fraud passes through
  - Audit + internal controls + segregation of duties: More covered
  - Solution: Stack independent constraints (if one fails, others still work)
  
- **Tumor suppression**:
  - Single tumor suppressor (p53) helps, but cells can evade
  - p53 + Rb + PTEN + others: Exponentially harder to evade
  - Solution: Cancer prevention = stacking independent constraints
  
- **System reliability**:
  - RAID 0: Single disk failure = total failure
  - RAID 1: Single disk failure = survives
  - RAID 6: Two disk failures = survives
  - Solution: Calculate maximum acceptable failure rate, stack constraints to exceed it

**Decision trigger**: "We need more safeguards" → Don't strengthen one, add another independent constraint

## PRINCIPLE 17: CONSTRAINT LANDSCAPE TOPOLOGY
**What it solves**: Why some optimization problems are easy and some are hard.

**Solutions**:
- **Hyperparameter search in ML**:
  - Smooth landscape = simple gradient descent finds solution
  - Rugged landscape with many local minima = must use special techniques
  - Solution: Test landscape topology first, choose optimization method after
  
- **Evolution in evolution**:
  - Smooth fitness landscape = smooth evolutionary progress
  - Rugged landscape = many species plateau, few breakthrough mutations make big jumps
  - Solution: Mutation rate should match landscape roughness
  
- **Problem-solving complexity**:
  - Sudoku with many solutions = easier (large solution basin)
  - Sudoku with one solution = harder (narrow solution basin)
  - Solution: Problem difficulty = basin structure, not just constraint count
  
- **Market competition**:
  - Market with many local optima = many successful companies can coexist
  - Market with single global optimum = winner-take-all
  - Solution: Understand market structure before entering

**Decision trigger**: "Why is this so hard to optimize?" → Look at landscape topology, not just constraint magnitude

## PRINCIPLE 18: INVERSE CONSTRAINT (Constraining Increases Output)
**What it solves**: Why more freedom sometimes decreases performance. Why rules help.

**Solutions**:
- **Meditation**:
  - Constraint: "Focus only on breath"
  - Outcome: Mental clarity, reduced anxiety (counter-intuitive)
  - Solution: Constraining attention improves well-being
  - Applicable: Focused attention increases performance in many domains
  
- **ML regularization**:
  - Constraint: "Keep model weights small"
  - Outcome: Better generalization (not on training data, but on new data)
  - Solution: Over-parameterized models need constraint to work
  - Can calculate optimal constraint strength
  
- **Error correction**:
  - Constraint: "Add redundancy"
  - Outcome: Can recover from errors (paradoxically, adds capacity despite seeming like limitation)
  - Solution: Redundancy is constraint that enables
  
- **Language grammar**:
  - Constraint: "Follow grammatical rules"
  - Outcome: Can communicate complex ideas (without grammar, communication constrained)
  - Solution: Rules enable information transfer
  
- **Legal systems**:
  - Constraint: "Property rights"
  - Outcome: More economic activity (not less)
  - Solution: Clear property constraints enable cooperation
  
- **Creative constraints**:
  - Constraint: "Write a story in 100 words"
  - Outcome: More creative than unlimited (not less)
  - Solution: Constraints force creativity

**Decision trigger**: "We're too constrained" → Try the inverse: What if constraint is the solution?

## WHAT THESE 18 PRINCIPLES ENABLE

### Diagnosis
- **Multi-constraint management**: Map ALL constraints before diagnosing
- **Information reading**: What is the constraint TELLING us?
- **Scale checking**: Is constraint appropriate for this scale?
- **Type identification**: What kind of constraint is this?

### Prediction
- **Cascade threshold**: Where will failure happen?
- **Nonlinear response**: Will this change be continuous or catastrophic?
- **Metastability**: Is this stable or fragile?
- **Coupling effects**: What will change when I adjust this?

### Optimization
- **Sweet spot finding**: Where is the optimal constraint level?
- **Distribution design**: How should constraints be distributed?
- **Precision tuning**: How precise does this need to be?
- **Efficiency trade**: Which routines to automate vs. improvise?

### Innovation
- **Constraint removal as exploration**: What constraints can we safely remove?
- **Inverse constraint**: Where can more constraint improve outcomes?
- **Temporal positioning**: What should we pre-position?
- **Redundancy stacking**: What independent safeguards do we need?

### Problem-solving
- **Constraint coupling**: What other constraints does this affect?
- **Landscape reading**: Is this an optimization problem or a structural problem?
- **Measurement choice**: What measurement reveals what we need to know?
