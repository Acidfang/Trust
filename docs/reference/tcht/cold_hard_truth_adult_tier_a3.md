# TIER A3 — GOVERNANCE
## Ensuring What You Built Stays Correct Without You

---

## Opening: The Problem That Comes After Scaling

You built something. It runs without you. Other people carry it. Systems hold under load. The direction persists when you are not present.

That is what A2 confirmed.

The problem that emerges after scaling is different. It is not about capacity or load. It is about alignment over time -- whether what you built continues to operate according to its original intent as it encounters new conditions, new people, new pressures, and new incentives.

Systems drift. Not because the people in them are malicious. Because every system develops local optimizations -- ways of operating that reduce friction and increase efficiency for the people inside it -- and some of those optimizations gradually diverge from the system's original intent.

This happens slowly. It happens through individually reasonable decisions. By the time it is visible, it has been running for a long time.

Governance is the capacity to maintain alignment between what the system does and what it was built to do -- without requiring your constant presence, and without reimposing central control that would undo the independence scaling produced.

That is a difficult problem. It requires signal -- the ability to see what is actually happening. It requires incentive design -- ensuring the system rewards the right behavior. It requires the correct balance between autonomy and control. It requires correction mechanisms that operate without central authority. And it requires addressing what happens when you are fully removed.

Each of those is a distinct failure mode. Each has its own routing.

### STATE A3.1 — SIGNAL

**Situation**

You are no longer inside the daily operation of the system. The question is whether you can still see what is actually happening -- not the reports that reach you, not the summaries that are constructed for your consumption, but the actual state of the system's behavior and alignment.

**OBSERVED:**
- Some information about the system reaches you
- The source and nature of that information can be described
- Whether that information accurately represents what is actually happening is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the information you receive is filtered, summarised, or constructed for your benefit
- Whether you have direct access to the system's actual outputs or only to reports about them
- Whether drift that has been occurring would be visible in the information you currently receive

**TENSION:**
- A system that only surfaces positive information produces invisible drift
- Reports constructed for a founder or leader tend to emphasise what is going well and minimise what is not
- The test of signal quality is whether it would surface a problem you would not want to hear

*If you arrived here carrying unresolved markers from prior tiers: the governance being attempted here is built on a foundation that has unresolved gaps. Those gaps are likely to affect what the system surfaces as signal.*

*Rule 2 applies. Loop check applies.*

**Your choices:**

A) The information you receive is sufficient. The people in the system would tell you if something was wrong
-- Signal assumed adequate without testing. You arrive at A3.2 [entry: signal assumed, drift undetected].

B) Test the signal quality. Seek direct access to system outputs alongside reported information. Check whether what you observe directly matches what is reported. Build signal sources that do not pass through people who have an interest in what you see
-- Signal quality confirmed through testing. You move to A3.2.

C) Reduce your involvement further. The system should be trusted to operate without your monitoring
-- Signal deliberately reduced. You arrive at A3.2 [entry: signal removed, governance blind].

**Notes:**
*What information reaches me about the system? Where does it come from? Would it surface a problem I would not want to hear? When did I last have direct access to what the system is actually producing?*

### STATE A3.2 — INCENTIVE STRUCTURE

**Situation**

The people in the system are responding to incentives -- what is rewarded, what is punished, what produces recognition, and what produces consequences. The question is whether those incentives are aligned with the system's direction or whether they are producing behavior that optimises for something else.

**OBSERVED:**
- The incentive structure in the system can be described
- The behaviors the incentives are producing can be observed
- Whether those behaviors align with the system's direction is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether any incentives are producing local optimisation at the expense of the system's overall direction
- Whether the incentive structure was designed explicitly or assembled by default
- Whether the people in the system understand what behavior is actually being rewarded

**TENSION:**
- Incentives designed by default reward what is easy to measure, not what matters
- Local optimisation -- doing what is rewarded within a part of the system -- often conflicts with the global intent of the system
- The incentive structure is more powerful than stated values -- people reliably do what is rewarded, not what is declared important

*If you arrived here [entry: signal assumed, drift undetected] from A3.1.A: incentive drift may already be occurring without your awareness. The examination here is happening without reliable signal about what behavior the incentives are actually producing.*

*If you arrived here [entry: signal removed, governance blind] from A3.1.C: there is no signal available about what incentives are producing. The examination here is theoretical.*

**Your choices:**

A) The incentive structure is appropriate. People understand what is expected and are generally performing well
-- Incentive alignment assumed without examination. You arrive at A3.3 [entry: incentive structure unexamined].

B) Map the actual incentives in the system -- what is rewarded, what produces consequences, what behavior is visible and recognised. Compare them to the behavior the direction requires. Redesign where the incentives are producing misalignment
-- Incentive structure examined and aligned. You move to A3.3.

C) Remove formal incentive structures. People in the system should be self-directed
-- Incentive structure removed without replacement. You arrive at A3.3 [entry: incentive vacuum].

**Notes:**
*What behavior does the current incentive structure actually reward? Is that the behavior the direction requires? Where is local optimisation occurring at the expense of the system's overall intent?*

### STATE A3.3 — AUTONOMY VS CONTROL

**Situation**

The system operates with some level of autonomy. The question is whether the current level of autonomy is appropriate -- enough to allow the system to function without becoming a bottleneck, but not so much that alignment cannot be maintained when decisions diverge from the direction.

**OBSERVED:**
- The current level of autonomy in the system can be described
- The types of decisions made autonomously versus escalated can be described
- Whether the autonomous decisions are consistently aligned with the direction is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the boundary between autonomous and escalated decisions is explicitly defined or implicit
- Whether autonomous decision-makers understand the direction well enough to apply it to situations the system was not explicitly designed for
- Whether the autonomy level is set by deliberate design or by the default reduction of your involvement

**TENSION:**
- Too little autonomy recreates dependency -- the system cannot operate without central direction and scaling collapses
- Too much autonomy without shared direction produces fragmentation -- parts of the system operate according to local interpretation rather than the original intent
- The correct level of autonomy is not a fixed point -- it changes as the capacity of people in the system develops

*If you arrived here [entry: incentive structure unexamined] from A3.2.A: incentives were not examined. The autonomous decisions being made here are responding to an unexamined incentive structure. The decisions may be locally rational but globally misaligned.*

*If you arrived here [entry: incentive vacuum] from A3.2.C: there is no incentive structure. Autonomous decisions here are being made without the behavioral guidance that incentives provide.*

**Your choices:**

A) The current autonomy level is appropriate. People know what to do
-- Autonomy level assumed appropriate without testing. You arrive at A3.4 [entry: autonomy level unconfirmed].

B) Define explicitly what decisions can be made autonomously and what requires escalation, based on the direction and the demonstrated capability of the people making decisions. Test whether autonomous decisions produce alignment. Adjust the boundary as capability develops
-- Autonomy level deliberately set and tested. You move to A3.4.

C) Increase autonomy to reduce bottlenecks. The system should be able to handle everything without escalation
-- Autonomy maximised without capability confirmation. You arrive at A3.4 [entry: autonomy exceeds confirmed capability].

**Notes:**
*What decisions are being made autonomously? Are they consistently aligned with the direction? Is the autonomy boundary explicit or implicit? Has it been tested?*

### STATE A3.4 — CORRECTION AT SCALE

**Situation**

Errors are occurring in the system. Some are visible. Some are not. The question is whether the system has correction mechanisms that operate without requiring central authority -- mechanisms that identify errors, trace them to source, and correct them at the level where they originate rather than at the surface.

**OBSERVED:**
- Errors in the system can be identified
- The current correction mechanisms can be described
- Whether those mechanisms operate without central involvement is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether errors are being identified at the point of origin or only when they produce visible consequences
- Whether correction is being applied at source or at the surface of individual errors
- Whether the correction mechanisms require your involvement or operate independently

**TENSION:**
- Correction that requires central authority recreates dependency at scale
- Surface correction without source tracing produces the same errors repeatedly under different forms
- A system that can only correct errors you are aware of is not self-correcting -- it is managed

*If you arrived here [entry: autonomy level unconfirmed] from A3.3.A: the autonomy level was not confirmed. Errors here may be occurring because autonomous decisions are outside the confirmed capability of those making them.*

*If you arrived here [entry: autonomy exceeds confirmed capability] from A3.3.C: autonomy exceeded confirmed capability. Error rates here may reflect that gap directly.*

**Your choices:**

A) Correct errors when they become visible. The system surfaces what needs attention
-- Reactive correction without source tracing. You arrive at A3.5 [entry: reactive correction only].

B) Build correction mechanisms that operate at the level where errors originate -- not waiting for visibility, but detecting early and correcting at source. Ensure those mechanisms operate without requiring your involvement
-- Self-correcting mechanisms established. You move to A3.5.

C) Reduce correction to allow the system to self-regulate naturally. Too much correction prevents adaptation
-- Correction reduced without replacement mechanism. You arrive at A3.5 [entry: correction deliberately reduced].

**Notes:**
*What correction mechanisms exist in the system? Do they operate without my involvement? Are they correcting at source or at surface? What errors are occurring that are not being caught?*

### STATE A3.5 — SUCCESSOR INTEGRITY

**Situation**

You are examining what happens when you are fully removed from the system -- not reduced involvement, but genuine absence. The question is whether the system continues to operate according to its original direction, or whether removal of the origin produces drift toward whatever is locally convenient.

**OBSERVED:**
- The system's operation without your involvement can be partially assessed
- The people and mechanisms that would carry the direction in your absence can be identified
- Whether those people and mechanisms would maintain direction integrity is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the direction is understood deeply enough by others to be applied to situations the system was not designed for
- Whether the succession of direction-carrying responsibility has been explicitly prepared or assumed
- Whether the system has been tested under conditions of your genuine absence, not just reduced involvement

**TENSION:**
- A direction that exists only in one person's understanding is not institutionalised -- it is contingent on that person
- Successor integrity requires deliberate preparation -- the direction must be transferred, not inherited by proximity
- The test of successor integrity is not whether others can describe the direction but whether they can apply it to situations you did not anticipate

*If you arrived here [entry: reactive correction only] from A3.4.A: the correction mechanisms require visibility of errors. In your absence, errors that do not produce immediate visible consequences will not be corrected.*

*If you arrived here [entry: correction deliberately reduced] from A3.4.C: correction was reduced. In your absence, errors that would have been caught by correction will accumulate.*

*Rule 4 applies at the prerequisite sheet. All markers from all tiers must be resolved.*

**Your choices:**

A) The people in the system understand the direction well enough to continue it. Succession will happen naturally
-- Succession assumed without preparation. You arrive at the prerequisite sheet [entry: succession assumed, integrity untested].

B) Prepare succession explicitly. Transfer the direction through deliberate engagement with those who will carry it. Test whether they can apply it to situations not previously encountered. Build the governance structures that will maintain direction integrity in your absence
-- Succession prepared and tested. You move to the Tier A3 Prerequisite Sheet.

C) The system should evolve its direction as conditions change. Maintaining the original direction too rigidly prevents necessary adaptation
-- Direction evolution accepted without distinguishing adaptation from drift. You arrive at the prerequisite sheet [entry: direction evolution vs drift unresolved].

**Notes:**
*Who carries the direction when I am absent? Have they been prepared explicitly or assumed by proximity? Have they been tested on situations I did not anticipate? What governance structures would maintain alignment in my permanent absence?*
