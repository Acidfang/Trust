# TIER YA1 — DIRECTION
## Stabilizing Your Path

---

## Opening: Why Direction Cannot Be Chosen

Direction is not something you pick. It is something that emerges from a specific sequence of events: real failure, honest tracing, pattern extraction, and enough repetition for the pattern to stabilize.

Most people try to skip this sequence. They want the direction without the failure. They want the pattern without the discomfort of extracting it. They want stability without the period of instability that precedes it.

That is not possible.

Failure is not the opposite of direction. It is the mechanism by which direction becomes real. A path that has never been tested by genuine failure is a hypothesis, not a direction. It feels like direction from the outside. Under real pressure, it collapses.

What this tier does is take the selection you made in Tier YA0 and test it against reality. Not to confirm it. To find out where it actually holds and where it does not. The places where it does not hold are the most useful information this tier produces. They are where the real pattern lives.

The dominant failure in this tier is not quitting after failure. It is failing without extracting the pattern -- cycling through the same failure repeatedly because the extraction step was skipped, and each new attempt starts from the same unexamined baseline.

This tier is designed to prevent that.

---

## A Note on How This Tier Works

There are four states in this tier.

YA1.3 -- Pattern Recognition -- is the most critical state in the book. It is where most people either develop genuine direction or enter a reset loop. The routing at that state is designed to make the distinction explicit.

All six system rules apply.

Entry markers from Tier YA0 persist here. Any unresolved markers -- including commitment depth gaps -- are still active and will affect what is available to you in this tier.

### STATE YA1.1 — FAILURE

**Situation**

You have engaged your selected domain at real stakes. Something did not work. The failure is real -- not a minor setback, but a genuine outcome that fell short of what was required and carried actual consequences. You are now in the aftermath.

**OBSERVED:**
- A real failure has occurred in your selected domain
- The consequences were real, not cushioned
- Your response to the failure can be described

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the failure was the result of your choices and actions, or external factors
- Whether you are reading the failure as information or as a verdict
- Whether the failure is being examined or avoided

**TENSION:**
- A failure read as a verdict produces exit or paralysis
- A failure read as information produces examination and adjustment
- The difference between these two readings is not the failure -- it is what you do with it

*If you arrived here carrying [entry: commitment depth gap] from YA0.4.C: commitment did not fully reach depth before this failure. The failure here may be partly a product of shallow commitment rather than genuine misalignment between you and the domain. That distinction matters for what you trace in YA1.2.*

*If you arrived here carrying [entry: default selection, no internal anchor] from YA0.3.A that was not resolved: the selection that led here was not internally anchored. The failure here may be telling you something about the selection, not just about your execution.*

*E2 entry note: if your prior environment consistently prevented real consequences, this may be the first genuinely unmanaged failure you have encountered. The discomfort of that is real and expected. It is not evidence that you cannot handle it.*

*Rule 2 applies. Loop check applies.*

**Your choices:**

A) The failure means this domain or path is not right. Exit and reconsider
-- Exit before examination. You return to YA0.3 [entry: post-failure exit, selection under review]. The selection must be re-examined before attempting this domain again.

B) Read the failure as information. Do not exit yet. Examine what specifically failed and why before deciding what it means
-- Failure engaged as information. You move to YA1.2.

C) Minimise the significance of the failure. It was circumstantial and does not reflect anything that needs to change
-- Failure externalised. You return to YA1.1 [entry: unexamined failure, pattern accumulating] when the same pattern produces the same outcome again.

**Notes:**
*What specifically failed? What were the consequences? Am I reading this as a verdict or as information?*

### STATE YA1.2 — CORRECTION

**Situation**

You are examining the failure. You are attempting to trace it to its source -- not to the nearest available cause, and not to external factors, but to the specific choice or pattern of choices that produced the outcome. This is the correction loop operating on your own behavior rather than on a task.

**OBSERVED:**
- The failure is being examined
- A likely source has been identified
- Whether that source is the actual source or the nearest available explanation is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the source identified is internal (your choices, your preparation, your execution) or external (circumstances, other people, conditions)
- Whether the correction being applied addresses the source or the symptom
- Whether the correction is being tested or only intended

**TENSION:**
- A correction applied to a symptom leaves the source intact
- An intended correction that is never tested produces no actual change
- Tracing to source requires honest examination of your own choices, which is harder than identifying external causes

*If you arrived here carrying [entry: commitment depth gap]: the trace here must account for whether shallow commitment contributed to the failure. If it did, the correction required includes addressing the commitment itself, not just the execution.*

*Loop check: if you have returned here more than once with [entry: unexamined failure] still active, apply Rule 3. The pattern is accumulating without being traced. Escalate to YA1.1 and engage the failure directly.*

**Your choices:**

A) Identify the most likely source and apply a correction without confirming it
-- Unconfirmed correction. The actual source remains active. You return to YA1.1 [entry: correction applied to wrong source] when the same pattern produces the same outcome.

B) Trace the failure backward to your own choices. Confirm the source is internal before applying the correction. Test the correction in a real situation before treating it as complete
-- Source confirmed, correction tested. You move to YA1.3.

C) The failure was primarily due to external factors. Adjust circumstances rather than behavior
-- External attribution. You return to YA1.1 [entry: external attribution, source untraced]. The pattern will repeat under different external circumstances.

**Notes:**
*What specifically in my choices produced this outcome? Have I confirmed the source is internal? What would a tested correction look like?*

### STATE YA1.3 — PATTERN RECOGNITION

**Situation**

You have failed and corrected more than once. You are now in a position to examine whether those failures share a common structure -- whether the same underlying pattern is producing different surface failures, or whether the failures are genuinely independent and the corrections are working.

This state is the most critical in the book. It is where reset loops are either broken or confirmed.

**OBSERVED:**
- Multiple failure and correction cycles have occurred
- The failures can be compared against each other
- Whether they share a common structure or are independent is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the corrections applied so far have addressed a common source or have each addressed independent symptoms
- Whether a pattern is visible across the failures or whether each one appears unique
- Whether the pattern being examined is about the domain itself or about your approach to any difficult domain

**TENSION:**
- A pattern that is not extracted produces an infinite correction loop -- different failures, same source, corrections that work temporarily and then recur in new form
- A pattern that is extracted but misread -- attributed to the domain when it belongs to the approach -- produces exit from a viable domain
- The critical distinction is whether the pattern is domain-specific or approach-specific

**How you arrived here determines what you are looking for:**

*If you arrived here [entry: correction applied to wrong source] from YA1.2.A more than once: the corrections have been applied to wrong sources repeatedly. The actual source has not been reached. The pattern extraction here must go deeper than previous attempts.*

*If you arrived here from YA1.2.B cleanly: corrections have been confirmed as accurate. Pattern recognition here is about identifying what the corrections reveal about your approach, not about finding an unresolved source.*

*If you arrived here [entry: external attribution, source untraced]: failures have been attributed externally without internal tracing. The pattern available here is incomplete. Return to YA1.2 and complete the internal trace before attempting pattern extraction.*

*Rule 3 applies if this state has been revisited more than once with the same marker unresolved -- this is the loop escape trigger point for this tier.*

**Your choices:**

A) The failures are all different. There is no common pattern. Each one was situational
-- Pattern denied. You return to YA1.1 [entry: pattern denied, loop continuing]. The same source will produce the same class of failure again.

B) Extract the common structure across the failures. Determine whether the pattern is domain-specific (suggesting genuine mismatch) or approach-specific (suggesting a correction to how you engage any difficult domain). Name the pattern explicitly before moving forward
-- Pattern extracted. You move to YA1.4.

C) The pattern is clear and it means this domain is not right. Exit and start over in a new domain
-- Pattern read as mismatch without confirming whether it is domain-specific or approach-specific. You return to YA0.3 [entry: pattern exit, approach unexamined]. If the pattern is approach-specific, the new domain will produce the same pattern.

**Notes:**
*What is common across the failures? Is the pattern about this domain specifically or about how I engage any difficult domain? Can I name it explicitly?*

### STATE YA1.4 — DIRECTION STABILIZATION

**Situation**

You have extracted a pattern. You have a picture of how you engage difficulty, where your approach holds, and where it does not. You are now assessing whether that picture is stable enough to constitute a direction -- something that can guide choices across multiple contexts -- or whether it is still too early and too narrow to be trusted as a direction.

**OBSERVED:**
- A pattern has been extracted from multiple failure and correction cycles
- A picture of your approach across difficulty has formed
- Whether that picture is stable and generalisable or situational and narrow is unclear

**UNRESOLVED:**
- Whether the pattern holds across different contexts or only within the specific domain where it was extracted
- Whether the direction emerging from the pattern is genuinely yours or is shaped by what has been available
- Whether you are willing to commit to a direction that is still incomplete rather than waiting for certainty

**TENSION:**
- A direction confirmed too early -- before enough cycles have occurred -- will not hold under significantly different conditions
- A direction deferred too long -- waiting for certainty that does not come -- produces continued drift
- The test is whether the pattern is stable across multiple independent observations, not whether it feels right

*If you arrived here carrying any unresolved markers from earlier in either tier: those markers represent gaps in the foundation this direction is being built on. A direction built on unresolved gaps will surface those gaps under pressure. Apply Rule 4 at the prerequisite sheet.*

*Loop check applies. If revisiting with same marker unresolved, apply Rule 3.*

**Your choices:**

A) The pattern is clear enough. Commit to the direction it suggests and move forward
-- Direction committed without confirming stability across contexts. You move to the Tier YA1 Prerequisite Sheet. Note: if the direction has only been tested within one context, this is a provisional commitment and the stability confirmation is still outstanding.

B) Test whether the pattern holds across at least two or three genuinely different contexts before treating the direction as stable. Observe whether the same pattern appears. If it does, the direction is real. If it does not, return to YA1.3 with the new information
-- Direction stabilized through cross-context confirmation. You move to the Tier YA1 Prerequisite Sheet.

C) The direction that is emerging is not what you expected or hoped for. Defer committing to it while you look for a direction that feels more aligned with your self-image
-- Direction deferred in favor of preferred self-image. You return to YA0.2 [entry: direction deferred, signal recheck needed]. The signal must be re-read without the self-image filter.

**Notes:**
*Does the pattern hold across different contexts or only in one? Is the emerging direction genuinely mine or am I looking for one that matches my self-image? Am I deferring because the direction is unclear or because it is uncomfortable?*
