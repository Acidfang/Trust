## DOMAIN 1: PHYSICS

### Constraint 1A: Speed of Light (c)
**What**: No information or matter can travel faster than 3×10⁸ m/s

**Ontological Root**: 
- Causality requires that causes precede effects
- If speeds exceeded c, effects could precede causes
- Therefore c must be ultimate speed limit to preserve causality order

**Why This Exists**:
- Not arbitrary physical law but consequence of spacetime structure
- Universe requires causal ordering to be coherent
- Therefore fundamental speed limit is prerequisite for existence

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Light speed in vacuum: constant regardless of observer
- Time dilation at high speeds
- Length contraction approaching c
- Energy requirements increase exponentially toward c

**Proper Navigation**:
- Accept c as hard limit for information transfer
- Design systems assuming light-speed communication delay (satellite networks, deep space probes)
- Use local computation instead of waiting for distant information
- Plan for increasing latency with distance

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Classical "action at distance" - assumed instantaneous effects → contradicts relativity → failed
- Quantum entanglement exploitation for faster-than-light signaling → impossible (no information transfer) → failed
- Wormholes as speed shortcuts → no known wormholes exist, violating energy conditions → fails

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Interplanetary probes plan communications accepting light-speed delays (Mars rover controlled with 20-minute round trip)
- Failure: Early space programs assumed radio was instantaneous → caused mission control errors

### Constraint 1B: Thermodynamic Arrow (Entropy)
**What**: Entropy increases in isolated systems; order decays to disorder

**Ontological Root**:
- Order (low entropy) is rare configuration
- Disorder (high entropy) is common configuration
- Universe has far more disordered states than ordered ones
- Therefore systems naturally drift toward disorder, not the reverse

**Why This Exists**:
- Not a law imposed on universe but statistical inevitability
- With 10²⁴ possible disordered states vs 1 ordered state, probability favors disorder
- Therefore entropy increase is consequence of large numbers, not external rule

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Heat flows from hot to cold, not reverse
- Closed systems become increasingly disorganized over time
- Energy becomes increasingly dispersed, unavailable for work
- Perpetual motion impossible

**Proper Navigation**:
- Accept that maintaining order requires energy expenditure
- Design systems to minimize energy waste (but not prevent it - impossible)
- Use entropy increase as energy source (internal combustion engines work because entropy increases)
- Plan for system degradation and budget for maintenance

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Perpetual motion machines (violate thermodynamics) → failed for 500 years
- Closed-loop systems with zero energy loss → impossible, any system will degrade → failed
- "Free energy" devices → ignore entropy requirement → failed
- Reversible processes at finite time → require infinite time → fails when time is limited

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Car engines use entropy increase (controlled combustion) as energy source
- Failure: Conspiracy theories about "suppressed" free energy → ignore fundamental limits → never work

### Constraint 1C: Planck Scale Limits
**What**: At distances < 10⁻³⁵ m and times < 10⁻⁴⁴ s, quantum gravity dominates; classical laws break down

**Ontological Root**:
- Planck scale emerges from combining three fundamental constants (G, ℏ, c)
- At this scale, quantum uncertainties in spacetime itself become significant
- Below this, spacetime structure itself becomes uncertain/undefined
- Therefore Planck scale represents limit of classical description

**Why This Exists**:
- Not arbitrary but emerges from deep structure of relativity + quantum mechanics
- Below this scale, need quantum gravity theory (not yet fully developed)
- Therefore this constraint represents boundary of known physics

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Can't measure distances below Planck length with classical methods
- Quantum uncertainty dominates below Planck time
- Spacetime foam and virtual particles proliferate at this scale
- Standard model physics fails as guide below this scale

**Proper Navigation**:
- Accept Planck scale as limit of current predictive power
- Use it as boundary condition for theories (physics valid above Planck scale)
- Look for new physics framework that works at/below Planck scale
- Don't use classical physics to predict Planck-scale phenomena

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Treating spacetime as continuous below Planck scale → fails when quantum effects dominate
- Ignoring quantum gravity → leads to infinities in calculations → unrenormalizable
- Assuming classical determinism at Planck scale → fails due to quantum uncertainty

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Standard model uses Planck scale as boundary where it's valid
- Failure: Early quantum field theory → infinities → required renormalization → showed need for new framework

### Constraint 1D: Conservation Laws
**What**: Energy, momentum, angular momentum, charge cannot be created/destroyed, only transformed

**Ontological Root**:
- Conservation laws emerge from symmetries (Noether's Theorem)
- Energy conservation = consequence of time symmetry
- Momentum conservation = consequence of space symmetry
- Charge conservation = consequence of gauge symmetry
- Therefore conservation laws are prerequisite for coherent physics

**Why This Exists**:
- If energy could be created/destroyed arbitrarily, prediction would be impossible
- Causality requires that changes have traceable causes
- Therefore conservation laws are requirement for deterministic physics

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Energy input = energy output + work done + heat dissipated
- No closed system can spontaneously produce energy
- Reactions must balance (chemical equations balance atoms)
- Isolated systems maintain total momentum

**Proper Navigation**:
- Use conservation laws as constraints in problem-solving (identify all energy flows)
- Design systems by tracking energy/momentum/charge flows
- Identify where energy is lost (entropy, heat, friction)
- Plan for required energy input to maintain order

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Assume energy is free/unlimited → violates conservation → system eventually fails
- Design without tracking energy flows → creates impossible situations → fails catastrophically
- Hope for spontaneous energy generation → violates physical law → never works

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Power grids designed by tracking energy flow from generation through consumption
- Failure: Economic systems that ignore energy constraints → eventually collapse (ecological economics)

### Constraint 1E: Wave-Particle Duality
**What**: All matter exhibits both particle and wave properties; can't observe both simultaneously

**Ontological Root**:
- Measurement necessarily interacts with system
- Measuring "where" particle is (particle property) destroys "what velocity" information (wave property)
- This is not limitation of current instruments but fundamental: interaction required for measurement

**Why This Exists**:
- Prerequisite for coherence: if things had perfectly defined position AND momentum simultaneously, causality breaks
- Therefore duality is consequence of requirement that reality be coherent and measurable

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Electrons show diffraction (wave property) when not observed
- Electrons show particle impacts when detector in place
- Can't get both properties simultaneously
- Heisenberg uncertainty: ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2

**Proper Navigation**:
- Choose which property you need to measure
- Accept you'll lose information about conjugate property
- Design experiments knowing you can't have both properties
- Use superposition while property is undetermined, collapse to one property when measured

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Try to measure both position and momentum simultaneously → fundamentally impossible
- Assume quantum randomness is measurement error → actually fundamental → fails to predict
- Try to have hidden variables restore determinism → Bell's theorem rules out local hidden variables

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Quantum computers use superposition (don't measure until final result)
- Failure: Early quantum experiments → contradictions → until accepting duality resolved them

## DOMAIN 2: BIOLOGY

### Constraint 2A: Metabolic Cost
**What**: Every living function requires energy expenditure; no free biological work

**Ontological Root**:
- Life = maintaining order (low entropy) against natural decay to disorder
- Maintaining order requires energy (Second Law of Thermodynamics)
- Therefore biology is constrained by thermodynamic requirements

**Why This Exists**:
- Organisms are low-entropy systems in high-entropy universe
- Maintaining this order requires constant energy input
- Therefore metabolic cost is not limitation but prerequisite for life

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Basal metabolic rate (minimum energy to stay alive)
- Growth requires more energy than maintenance
- Reproduction requires energy investment in offspring
- No biological process runs on zero energy

**Proper Navigation**:
- Accept metabolic cost as fundamental constraint on what organism can do
- Energy budget determines possible activities (trade-offs: growth vs reproduction, activity vs rest)
- Design life history assuming energy scarcity
- Optimize for energy efficiency in critical functions

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Try to produce more offspring than energy allows → starvation → population crashes
- Design organisms without energy budget → violates thermodynamics → fails
- Assume organisms can maintain fitness while starved → violates biology → fails

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Migratory animals budget energy for long journeys (reduce activity, burn fat stores)
- Failure: Invasive species introduced to islands without predators → reproduce beyond carrying capacity → ecosystem collapse

---

### Constraint 2B: Genetic Heritability Limit
**What**: You can only pass on genes you possess; cannot design from scratch each generation

**Ontological Root**:
- Reproduction requires copying existing information
- Copying is imperfect (mutations occur) but must start from existing blueprint
- Therefore evolution works with existing genes, modifying them incrementally
- Cannot jump to radically different designs without intermediate steps

**Why This Exists**:
- Information must come from somewhere
- Only source of variation is mutation (random changes) + selection
- Therefore life is constrained by its own history

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Evolution is path-dependent (current forms limited by evolutionary history)
- Humans have constraints from being mammals, mammals from vertebrates, etc.
- We cannot redesign ourselves de novo; only modify existing blueprint
- Vestigial structures remain because evolution can't undo, only modify

**Proper Navigation**:
- Understand that organisms are constrained by evolutionary history
- Use selective breeding working with existing variation
- Understand that radical improvements require many generations (slow)
- Design agriculture around organisms' actual capacities, not idealized versions

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Try to create organism with radically new function without intermediate steps → requires information source → fails
- Ignore genetic constraints when breeding for traits → get unintended side effects (pleiotropy)
- Assume organisms are infinitely plastic → underestimate time for adaptation

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Selective breeding of dogs over thousands of years → achieved huge variation within species
- Failure: Trying to breed for all desired traits simultaneously → trade-offs make it impossible

### Constraint 2C: Niche Saturation
**What**: Every ecological niche has maximum carrying capacity; beyond this, population crashes

**Ontological Root**:
- Organisms require resources (food, space, water)
- Resources are finite
- Therefore population is constrained by resource availability
- Cannot exceed carrying capacity indefinitely

**Why This Exists**:
- Resources = bounded (prerequisite for existence)
- Organisms = consumers of resources
- Therefore population bounded by resources

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Population growth logistic (exponential initially, then levels off)
- Resource depletion with overpopulation
- Disease and starvation increase at high population density
- Boom-bust cycles when carrying capacity exceeded

**Proper Navigation**:
- Identify carrying capacity of ecosystem before settling/introducing species
- Plan population growth assuming eventual saturation
- Maintain population slightly below carrying capacity for stability
- Build in redundancy/buffer for unpredictable resource changes

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Assume population can grow indefinitely → exhausts resources → crash
- Ignore carrying capacity → overshoot → collapse
- Maintain population exactly at carrying capacity → no buffer → one bad year causes crash

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Sustainable fisheries that harvest below carrying capacity maintain fish stocks
- Failure: Overfishing → stock collapse → recovery takes decades (if at all)

## DOMAIN 3: COGNITION

### Constraint 3A: Working Memory Limit
**What**: Conscious mind can hold ~7±2 items simultaneously

**Ontological Root**:
- Consciousness is limited resource
- Neural bandwidth for conscious attention is finite
- Therefore working memory is bounded, not unlimited

**Why This Exists**:
- Prerequisite for identity: unified conscious experience requires bounded scope
- If consciousness had infinite capacity, wouldn't be unified experience
- Therefore bounded working memory is requirement for coherent consciousness

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Can hold ~7 digits in mind simultaneously
- Larger numbers require writing them down or chunking
- Multitasking causes performance degradation (switching attention is costly)
- Complexity limit: can't consciously track more than ~7 relationships simultaneously

**Proper Navigation**:
- External memory (write things down, use tools) to extend working memory
- Chunking: group items into meaningful units to increase effective capacity
- Sequential processing: process items one at a time, store results
- Simplify complex problems by breaking into smaller chunks

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Try to hold 20 items consciously → exceed capacity → items get lost
- Multitask on equally complex tasks → attention thrashing → performance worse than sequential
- Assume humans can track complex system state without tools → exceed capacity → make errors

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Expert chess players chunk board patterns into "meaningful units" (not individual squares)
- Failure: Trying to multiply large numbers mentally → exceed working memory → errors

---

### Constraint 3B: Attention Bottleneck
**What**: Cannot consciously attend to two complex tasks simultaneously

**Ontological Root**:
- Consciousness has single focus of attention
- Two complex tasks compete for this single resource
- Therefore cannot perform both simultaneously with full attention

**Why This Exists**:
- Unified conscious experience requires single attentional focus
- Multiple simultaneous foci would fragment consciousness
- Therefore bottleneck is consequence of consciousness being unified

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Can't read two books simultaneously and understand both
- Driving + phone conversation → both suffer degradation
- Error rates increase exponentially with multitasking complexity
- Automatic tasks (breathing, balance) don't require conscious attention

**Proper Navigation**:
- Automate simple tasks (practice until unconscious) to free attention
- Prioritize: do high-attention tasks sequentially, low-attention in parallel
- Use external tools (alarms, checklists) instead of conscious monitoring
- Schedule: block time for focused work without interruption

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- Expect people to consciously monitor multiple complex systems → attention fails → errors catastrophic
- Multitask on important high-complexity tasks → quality degrades → failures increase
- Ignore attention bottleneck in system design → human error in critical systems

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Cockpit design automates many functions, alerts pilot to critical issues → pilot can attend to core flying
- Failure: Air traffic controllers tasked with too many simultaneous complex decisions → missed alerts → accidents

### Constraint 3C: Long-term Consolidation Bottleneck
**What**: Memory consolidation requires time; cannot instantly memorize infinite information

**Ontological Root**:
- Memory requires physical changes in neural structure
- Physical changes require time to occur
- Therefore memory encoding is time-limited, not instantaneous

**Why This Exists**:
- Learning = changing neural structure
- Neural changes are biological processes (require protein synthesis, synapse formation)
- Therefore learning has minimum time requirement

**Physical Manifestation**:
- Sleep required for memory consolidation
- Spaced repetition more effective than cramming
- New learning interferes with recent learning (interference theory)
- Muscle memory (motor learning) requires repeated practice over time

**Proper Navigation**:
- Plan learning schedule with spaced repetition (spacing effect)
- Prioritize sleep and rest for memory consolidation
- Practice skills repeatedly to build robust long-term memory
- Don't expect instant expertise (requires time for consolidation)

**Improper Attempts** (Why they fail):
- All-night cramming → interferes with consolidation → knowledge doesn't stick
- Expect expertise without practice → haven't done consolidation yet → fails under stress
- Skip sleep to learn more → impairs consolidation → learn less, not more

**Real Examples**:
- Success: Expert musicians practice regularly over years → consolidate motor learning → can perform reliably
- Failure: Students cram before exam → doesn't consolidate → forget quickly after test

**Constraint 3D: Learning Curve Limitations**
- Early learning is fast (low effort), late learning is slow (high effort)

- Improper: Expect linear learning rates throughout skill development

- Proper: Automate stable skills, keep variable skills conscious

- Cosmic microwave background limits (can't see earlier than ~380,000 years after Big Bang)
- Large-scale structure constraints (universe large-scale structure is sparse)

- Sleep necessity (can't sustain wakefulness indefinitely)
- Aging rate (can't accelerate/decelerate cellular aging significantly)
- Injury recovery time (wounds need time to heal, can't skip)
- Biological sex constraints (reproductive constraints differ by sex)

## SYNTHESIS: META-PATTERN

Every domain has:

1. **Fundamental Constraints** (irreducible physical/logical limits)
2. **Ontological Roots** (why constraints must exist for reality to cohere)
3. **Proper Navigation** (working WITH constraints, not against)
4. **Improper Attempts** (why fighting constraints fails)
5. **Real Evidence** (actual examples of success and failure)

The pattern: **Constraints are not bugs in reality. They are the structure that makes reality possible.**

Proper thinking: Identify constraints, understand their roots, optimize within them.
Improper thinking: Ignore constraints, waste effort fighting them, fail when reality enforces limits.
