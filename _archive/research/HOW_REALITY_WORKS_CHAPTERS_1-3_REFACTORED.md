### 3.1: The Era of Stars and Stellar Nucleosynthesis

After recombination (T = 380,000 years), the universe becomes transparent and dark.

**Star Formation: Gravity Follows Gradient**

In gas clouds, density varies slightly (from quantum fluctuations stretched by inflation).

Billions of stars form. Each star is gravity's solution to: "How can diffuse gas lower its potential energy?"

Answer: Collapse into dense, hot object.

**Inside the Star: Stellar Nucleosynthesis**

In a star's core:
- Temperature: 10 million Kelvin (core of Sun)
- Pressure: Immense (core of Sun: 250 billion atmospheres)

At these conditions, hydrogen nuclei (protons) collide hard enough to fuse:

**4 Protons → 1 Helium Nucleus + Energy (+ neutrinos)**

This is the proton-proton chain.

Energy released (photons, neutrinos) supports the star against gravity. The star reaches equilibrium—gravity pulling inward balanced by radiation pressure pushing outward.

**As the star ages:**

Helium accumulates in the core. For a low-mass star (like our Sun), fusion stops when hydrogen is exhausted.

For high-mass stars:
- After hydrogen: Fuse helium → Carbon and Oxygen
- After helium: Fuse carbon → Neon and Magnesium
- After carbon: Fuse oxygen → Silicon and sulfur
- ...continues building heavier elements...
- Until: Iron forms (iron fusion requires energy input, not energy release)

When iron core reaches critical mass, fusion cannot continue. The core collapses catastrophically.

**The Supernova: Explosive Element Synthesis**

The iron core collapses so fast it rebounds like a spring.

Energy released in explosion is utterly stupendous (~10^44 joules).

In that explosion:
- Elements heavier than iron are synthesized (gold, uranium, platinum)
- All heavy elements are blasted into space
- Stellar material disperses into interstellar medium

**Result:** Universe is enriched with new elements.

Primordial: Only H, He, trace Li
After first stars: H, He, Li, C, N, O, Ne, Mg, Si, Fe, Ni
After supernovae: Plus all heavy elements

This enrichment is essential. Without it, **no chemistry is possible**. Without chemistry, **no life is possible**.
