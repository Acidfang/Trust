# Politics as Collective Coherence

Political systems are energy allocation mechanisms. Revolutions occur when environmental density (ρ) spikes faster than institutions can adapt (k). Institutional lifespan is determined by their r² (bureaucratic complexity) and available E (legitimacy/resources).

---

## The Core Principle

Governments pay E = k · ρ · r² to maintain social coherence. When external density (resource scarcity, war, disruption) rises, institutions must adapt or collapse. Revolution timing is predictable from ρ surges.

**Key insight:** Revolutions are not ideological. They're thermodynamic failures.

## Key Insights

### Revolutions as E-Failure Events
- Institutions maintain legitimacy by providing stability (low ρ, high E)
- When external ρ spikes (famine, invasion, economic collapse), institutions cannot adapt fast enough
- Population's E (confidence in system) depletes
- System reorganizes (revolution) or collapses (state failure)
- Timing: 1-2 years after ρ-spike, when initial adaptation responses fail

### Institutional Decay as r² Growth
- Governments expand reach (r) to handle new territories/populations
- Coordination cost scales as r²
- Eventually r² cost > available E (resources, legitimacy)
- Result: Institutional fragmentation, outsourcing, decentralization
- Pattern: Every major empire shows this collapse curve

### Moral Systems Track ρ
- Low ρ (abundant resources): Cooperation valued, redistribution, altruism
- High ρ (scarcity): Competition valued, hierarchy, nationalism
- This is not moral relativism. This is rational adaptation to environmental conditions
- Same society shows different "moral values" in boom vs. crisis

### Corruption as Rational Adaptation
- When ρ high and E (official resources) low: corruption emerges as alternative E source
- Low-corruption societies have high official E and low ρ
- Corruption is not moral failure; it's resource reallocation when formal systems fail
