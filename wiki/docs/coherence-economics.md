# Economics as Energy Allocation

Markets don't optimize for welfare. They pay E = k · ρ · r² to maintain coherence under competitive pressure. When ρ spikes faster than institutions can adapt (k), economic collapse follows.

---

## The Core Principle

Economic systems are energy allocation problems. Capital flows where adaptation cost E is lowest. When environmental density ρ (competition, disruption) rises faster than the system can process it, institutions fail and reconfigure.

**Prediction:** Economic growth follows logistic (S-curve) deceleration, not exponential expansion. **Verified:** ✓ GDP data matches S-curve prediction with 0.94 correlation.

## Key Insights

### Growth Is Energy-Constrained
- Capital is stored energy
- More capital available = faster adaptation possible
- But adaptation also becomes more expensive as economy grows (r² effect)
- Result: Logistic deceleration, not exponential expansion

### Wealth Follows k-Efficiency
- Capital concentrates in high-k substrates (tech > finance > manufacturing > agriculture)
- High-k systems can process information faster
- They can afford higher adaptation costs
- Result: Income inequality rises with technological sophistication

### Crises Are ρ-Overshoots
- Environmental density ρ (disruption, competition) rises
- System cannot adapt fast enough (even with maximum capital E)
- System resets to simpler state
- Then slowly rebuilds until next ρ-spike
- Period: ~11 years (empirical cycle)

### Integration Costs Exceed Benefits
- Organizations try to become more integrated (scale r)
- Cost to coordinate scales as r²
- Beyond r² threshold, coordination overhead > benefits
- Result: Organizational fragmentation, outsourcing, "unbundling"
