# Gate 3 Discovery Template: Mastery Through Iteration

## For Yourself

### The 100 Attempts Challenge

Pick a skill you want to develop but have told yourself you're "not good at":
- Writing
- Tennis/badminton
- Drawing
- Programming
- A musical instrument
- Speaking a language

**The Challenge:**
Commit to 100 deliberate attempts over 12 weeks (roughly 8-10 per week).

**Tracking:**
- Attempt 1-10: Note baseline, difficulty, emotion
- Attempt 11-20: What's different? Is iteration helping?
- Attempt 21-50: Track improvement. Is it real?
- Attempt 51-100: Can you see the trajectory? Is mastery happening?

**Reflection Points:**
- Week 1: "This is hard. I'm probably not talented."
- Week 4: "Wait, I can see improvement."
- Week 8: "I'm significantly better than week 1."
- Week 12: "This is how mastery actually happens."

### Key Insight

Gate 3 passes when you **viscerally understand** that quality emerges from quantity.

Not intellectually ("Yeah, practice is important").
But actually: "Oh. I can see my own improvement over attempts. Competence is produced by iteration."

## For Parenting

### The Setup: No Praise for Potential

**What NOT to do:**
- Praise for being "natural" ("You're so smart!")
- Praise after success ("Great job!")
- Praise that makes effort feel unnecessary

**What TO do:**
- Notice effort: "I see you tried it a different way."
- Notice iteration: "That's attempt 7. You found something new in it."
- Notice persistence: "You stayed with this even when it was hard."

### The Progression: Multi-Attempt Expectation

**For younger children (6-10):**
- Puzzles (expect many attempts; no "solving it quickly" = smart)
- Drawing/building (iteration is the point, not the final product)
- Learning to ride a bike (expect 50+ attempts; falling is learning)

**Key conversation:**
"This is hard. That means your brain is working. That means you're learning."
(Not: "You'll get it eventually." Worse: "Maybe this isn't for you.")

**For older children (10-16):**
- Real projects with real difficulty
- Set expectation: "This will take iteration."
- Track: After attempt 5, 10, 20 — what's different?
- Celebrate: "Your 30th attempt looks different than your 1st."

### What You're Teaching

The child discovers: "My 30th attempt is better than my 1st. My 50th is better than my 30th. If I stay with difficulty, I get better."

Not from being told. From living it.

## For Teams/Organizations

### Minimal Viable Gate 3: The Iteration Requirement

Pick one project. Explicit rule: **First attempt is not the submission.**

**The constraint:**
- Minimum 3 iterations before final
- Each iteration must be materially different
- Team reviews improvements between iterations

**Week 1:**
- First attempt (rough, rough is OK)
- Review: What would we do differently?

**Week 2:**
- Second attempt (specifically addressing iteration 1 gaps)
- Review: What else emerged?

**Week 3:**
- Third attempt (refined; ready to ship)

**Observation:**
- Team notices: Quality happens through iteration, not inspiration
- Mistakes become learning, not failure
- Competence is visible as iterative improvement

## Troubleshooting

**"They're just repeating the same thing 3 times"**
→ That's not iteration. Iteration requires reflection/change.
→ Guide: "What did you notice about attempt 1? Let's intentionally do something different in attempt 2."

**"They want to quit after attempt 3"**
→ That's the scaffolding talking: "If it's still hard, I'm not good."
→ Counter: "Professional musicians do 1,000 attempts. Writers do 50 drafts. You're at 3. Let's get to 10."

**"I'm not good at iteration either"**
→ Perfect. Do it alongside them. Show them your 10 attempts at something and the improvement.
→ The gate passes faster when they see adults actually doing it.

## When Gate 3 Is Fully Passed

Children/adults:
- Embrace difficulty as learning signal
- Stay with attempts longer
- See improvement over time
- Understand: competence = iteration, not talent
- Ready for Gate 5 (consequence navigation)

---

## The Long Game

Gate 3 is foundational for everything else. A person who has passed Gate 3 will:
- Learn new skills throughout life
- Not quit when things are hard
- Understand that expertise is built, not born
- Have resilience in the face of difficulty

This is why nations that treat education as "mastery through iteration" (Finland, Japan) produce more competent adults than nations that treat it as "success = first-try performance."
