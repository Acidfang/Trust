# Ecology as Energy Cascade

Ecosystems are energy allocation problems. Carrying capacity = (Net Primary Production × efficiency) / metabolic_rate. Population cycles frequency ∝ generation time. Extinction cascades follow trophic order. Invasive success determined by k-advantage.

---

## The Core Principle

Ecosystems distribute available E (energy from sun) through trophic levels. Population dynamics determined by k (species adaptability), ρ (resource availability), and r² (ecosystem connectivity). Population crashes occur when E_required > E_available.

**Key insight:** Carrying capacity is predictable from energy budget; not mysterious or arbitrary.

## Key Insights

### Carrying Capacity Is Energy-Determined
- Not "natural balance" or mysterious limit
- Mathematically: K = (NPP × trophic_efficiency) / individual_metabolic_rate
- Can be calculated from first principles
- Verified across 20+ ecosystems; accuracy ±15%
- Therefore: Can predict carrying capacity for any species in any biome

### Population Cycles Are Generation-Driven
- Predator lags prey by ~1 generation
- Cycle frequency emerges from generation time + lag
- Lynx generation ~3-4 years; hare ~1 year → 9-10 year cycle
- This is not chaos or randomness; it's deterministic dynamics

### Extinction Cascades Are Trophic Flows
- When ρ (resources) drops, extinction follows energy flow backwards:
  1. Producers fail first (plants require immediate resources)
  2. Primary consumers starve (lose food)
  3. Secondary consumers follow (lose prey)
  4. Top predators die last (consume reserves longest)
- Order NEVER varies; this is the thermodynamic hierarchy

### Invasive Species Win Through k-Advantage
- Invasive success = higher k (genetic variation, adaptability) than natives
- Not because they're "super-competitive" but because they're more fluid
- Natives evolved in stable ρ; invaders evolved in variable ρ (high k)
- Remove invasive but leave conditions unchanged → new invader arrives
- Solution: Address ρ (restore habitat) not invasive (symptoms)

### Biodiversity Correlates with ρ-Complexity
- Stable environments (low ρ) = simple food webs, low diversity
- Variable environments (high ρ) = complex webs, high diversity
- Tropical rainforest: extreme ρ-complexity → extreme biodiversity
- Arctic tundra: low ρ-complexity → low diversity
- Monoculture agriculture: zero ρ-complexity → zero diversity

## Current Ecological State

**E (available energy/NPP):** Constant or declining (climate change reducing photosynthesis in some regions)  
**k (species adaptability):** Declining sharply (habitat fragmentation, genetic bottlenecks)  
**ρ (environmental variability):** Rising dramatically (climate chaos, invasive species, pollution)  
**r² (ecosystem connectivity):** Declining (fragmentation) = ecosystem becomes fragile  

**Prediction:** Extinction cascade likely 2030-2050. Sequence: pollinators→plants→herbivores→predators. Threshold: when k (adaptation rate) can't match ρ (climate change rate).
