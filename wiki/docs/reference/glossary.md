# Complete Glossary

A unified glossary of key terms used throughout the wiki. Terms are organized by system, with cross-system connections noted.

**Tip:** Use Ctrl+F to search this glossary for any term.

---

## System 1: Human Development

### **Agency**
The capacity to be the actor in one's own life—to make decisions and experience the direct consequences of those decisions. Agency is foundational; without it, people cannot develop genuine competence. When agency is removed or prevented, development stops.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 1)
- **Example:** A child who makes a mistake and experiences the natural consequence learns more than a child who is prevented from making the mistake
- **Related terms:** [Responsibility](#responsibility), [Consequence Management](#consequence-management), [Internal Coherence](#internal-coherence)

### **Gate** (Developmental Gate)
One of 10 sequential thresholds that humans must pass through to develop genuine competence. Gates cannot be skipped. Passing through a gate requires encountering difficulty, failing, and recovering. Each gate builds on previous ones.
- **Used in:** System 1 (entire framework)
- **Example:** Gate 1 (Agency) must be passed before Gate 2 (Responsibility) can be meaningfully passed
- **Related terms:** [Agency](#agency), [Scaffolding](#scaffolding), [Help Systems](#help-system-the-paradox)

### **Goal-Blindness**
The inability to see past one's own goals to the effects those goals have on others. A helper's goal is "help," which blinds them to "preventing gate-passage." Parents' goal is "protect child," which blinds them to "preventing agency development."
- **Used in:** System 1 (central concept)
- **Example:** A teacher trying to help struggling students by making assignments easier prevents the students from passing through Gate 3 (Complexity Navigation)
- **Related terms:** [Help System (The Paradox)](#help-system-the-paradox), [Dependency](#dependency)

### **Help System (The Paradox)**
A system designed to "help" that actually prevents the development it claims to enable. By removing difficulties, providing shortcuts, and preventing consequences, help systems keep people dependent and incompetent. The paradox: more help = less competence.
- **Used in:** System 1 (central observation)
- **Example:** Extensive therapy/coaching without real-world consequences often produces people MORE dependent on external help, not less
- **Related terms:** [Goal-Blindness](#goal-blindness), [Scaffolding](#scaffolding), [Development](#development)

### **Internal Coherence**
The state where a person's actions, beliefs, experience, and results align consistently. When someone has internal coherence, they can predict outcomes, learn from experience, and adjust based on feedback. Incoherence produces confusion and prevents learning.
- **Used in:** System 1 (central outcome)
- **Example:** Person A: "I try hard, things work out"→ coherent. Person B: "I try hard, random things happen" → incoherent
- **Related terms:** [Development](#development), [Consequence Management](#consequence-management), [Causality Understanding](#causality-understanding)

### **Scaffolding** 
Temporary supports that help someone accomplish a task they couldn't do alone. Scaffolding is MEANT to be removed as competence grows. When scaffolding stays in place, it prevents gate-passage and creates dependency.
- **Used in:** System 1 (mechanism of help systems)
- **Example:** Training wheels on a bike are scaffolding (meant to come off). Removing them lets children learn balance
- **Related terms:** [Help System (The Paradox)](#help-system-the-paradox), [Dependency](#dependency)

### **Responsibility**
The acknowledgment that one's actions produce one's results. Responsibility is not blame; it's clarity about causality. Without responsibility, people cannot learn from experience or develop genuine competence.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 2)
- **Example:** "My report was rejected because I did poor research" (responsibility) vs. "My report was rejected because the boss doesn't like me" (no responsibility)
- **Related terms:** [Agency](#agency), [Causality Understanding](#causality-understanding), [Consequence Management](#consequence-management)

### **Complexity Navigation**
The ability to encounter a difficult task, fail, iterate, learn, and eventually master it. This is Gate 3. People who skip this gate believe "I should get it right first try" and fail at anything challenging.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 3)
- **Example:** Learning to code: iteration, failure, debugging, improvement until competence is reached
- **Related terms:** [Pattern Recognition](#pattern-recognition), [Consequence Management](#consequence-management)

### **Pattern Recognition**
The ability to see structures that repeat across different domains. Gate 4. Enables seeing "help systems fail the same way everywhere" or "this management problem is the same as that parenting problem."
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 4)
- **Example:** Seeing that "removing difficulty" produces the same result (dependency) in parenting, education, management, and therapy
- **Related terms:** [Binary Foundation](#binary-foundation), [Election (Physics)](#election)

### **Consequence Management**
The ability to live with the results of one's choices and extract learning from them. Gate 5. Without this, people externalize blame or avoid decisions entirely.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 5)
- **Example:** A business decision produces a loss; the owner learns what went wrong instead of blaming market conditions
- **Related terms:** [Responsibility](#responsibility), [Agency](#agency)

### **Source Verification**
The ability to evaluate where information comes from and whether it's trustworthy. Gate 6. Prevents both credulity (believing everything) and cynicism (believing nothing).
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 6)
- **Example:** Understanding the difference between "my friend told me" (hearsay) and "research shows" (method) and "I tested it myself" (direct evidence)
- **Related terms:** [Causality Understanding](#causality-understanding), [Binary Logic](#binary-logic)

### **Temporal Continuity**
The sense that one's life has coherent continuity over time—past informs present, present creates future. Gate 7. People without this feel fractured or "stuck repeating patterns."
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 7)
- **Example:** "I made this mistake before, I learned from it, I won't make it again" vs. "This keeps happening to me randomly"
- **Related terms:** [Internal Coherence](#internal-coherence), [Causality Understanding](#causality-understanding)

### **Causality Understanding**
The ability to trace effects back to causes. "This happened because..." Gate 8. Enables learning from experience and making better decisions. Without it, the world seems random.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 8)
- **Example:** Understanding "my sales are down because my process changed" vs. "sales are down and I don't know why"
- **Related terms:** [Responsibility](#responsibility), [Consequence Management](#consequence-management)

### **Self-Correction**
The willingness to change beliefs and behaviors when evidence shows they're not working. Gate 9. Without this gate, people become rigid and defensive.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 9)
- **Example:** "My approach isn't working; I'll try something different" vs. "My approach is fine; the problem is everyone else"
- **Related terms:** [Internal Coherence](#internal-coherence), [Learning Capacity](#learning-capacity)

### **Integration**
The ability to hold multiple truths simultaneously without collapsing them into contradiction or false hierarchy. Gate 10. The ultimate gate; enables wisdom.
- **Used in:** System 1 (Gate 10)
- **Example:** "Help is sometimes needed AND helping can create dependency" (both true, not contradictory)
- **Related terms:** [Coherence](#coherence), [Pattern Recognition](#pattern-recognition)

### **Development** (Genuine)
The process of passing through all 10 gates via direct experience, consequence, and integration. Results in internal coherence, genuine competence, and capacity to handle real problems without external support.
- **Used in:** System 1 (entire framework)
- **Example:** A person who has passed through all 10 gates can handle unexpected problems, learn, adapt, and thrive
- **Related terms:** [Internal Coherence](#internal-coherence), [Gate](#gate)

### **Dependency**
The state where a person cannot function without external support. Created by help systems that prevent gate-passage. Dependency is not "help"; it's the opposite of development.
- **Used in:** System 1 (observation of help systems)
- **Example:** A person who needs a therapist to make every decision, unlike a person who uses therapy to build decision-making capacity
- **Related terms:** [Help System (The Paradox)](#help-system-the-paradox), [Scaffolding](#scaffolding)

## System 2: Zero-Error Computing

### **Binary Logic**
The foundation of all computation: two states (0/1, true/false, on/off) that combine into all possible complexity. Thinking binary means reducing complexity to fundamental distinctions.
- **Used in:** System 2 (foundation), System 3 (physics)
- **Example:** Any decision can be reduced to a binary choice; any complex system can be described as combinations of simple binary operations
- **Related terms:** [Distinction](#distinction), [Binary Foundation](#binary-foundation), [Verification](#verification)

### **Verification**
The process of checking whether logic is correct while thinking about it, before code execution. The core principle of System 2: you can verify your thinking without running it.
- **Used in:** System 2 (entire framework)
- **Example:** Walking through code logic: "If X happens, then Y must follow" → Check: Does Y actually follow? Yes/No
- **Related terms:** [Binary Logic](#binary-logic), [Zero-Error Mandate](#zero-error-mandate)

### **Zero-Error Mandate**
The principle: If you've thought through the logic completely, code should work on first execution. Errors in code indicate errors in thinking that weren't caught during verification.
- **Used in:** System 2 (central principle)
- **Example:** Function planned: input A → check A exists → process → output B. If code fails, it means the thinking didn't cover all cases
- **Related terms:** [Verification](#verification), [Completeness](#completeness)

### **Decision Logger**
A tool that records the "why" behind each decision point in code. Later, when something fails, you can review the decisions that led there and identify where thinking went wrong.
- **Used in:** System 2 (tool)
- **Example:** "At this point, I decided to assume input was always valid. That assumption failed when invalid input appeared."
- **Related terms:** [Verification](#verification), [Debugging](#debugging)

### **Duplicate Detector**
A tool that catches when the same logic is implemented in multiple places. Duplicated logic means future bugs in one place won't be fixed in the other; it's a sign of incomplete thinking.
- **Used in:** System 2 (tool)
- **Example:** Validation logic in two places that should be one shared function
- **Related terms:** [Verification](#verification), [Completeness](#completeness)

### **Pre-Commit Hook**
An automated check that runs before code is committed. It can verify: no duplicates, decisions are logged, logic paths are complete. Forces thinking to be done before code reaches production.
- **Used in:** System 2 (tool)
- **Example:** Hook runs: "Checking for duplicates... found 3... BLOCKED. Fix duplicates before commit"
- **Related terms:** [Verification](#verification), [Completeness](#completeness)

### **Mandate** (System 2)
A binding rule that structures thinking: "Before writing code, you must verify thinking by checking: [X], [Y], [Z]." Non-negotiable. Prevents most bugs before they occur.
- **Used in:** System 2 (framework)
- **Example:** Mandate: "Before writing any function, map all possible inputs and verify the function handles every case"
- **Related terms:** [Zero-Error Mandate](#zero-error-mandate), [Verification](#verification)

### **Completeness**
The state where every possible case is accounted for and handled. Incomplete thinking leaves gaps where bugs hide. Verified completeness means no hidden paths.
- **Used in:** System 2 (core quality)
- **Example:** Function: "If role is admin, do X. If role is user, do Y." Question: What if role is null? Incomplete until that's handled
- **Related terms:** [Verification](#verification), [Binary Logic](#binary-logic)

### **Debugging**
The process of finding where thinking went wrong by tracing the path from failure back to source. With proper logging and verification, debugging becomes obvious rather than mysterious.
- **Used in:** System 2 (outcome of incomplete thinking)
- **Example:** "Code failed at line 50. Decision log shows why that decision was made. Reviewing that decision, I see the assumption was wrong."
- **Related terms:** [Decision Logger](#decision-logger), [Verification](#verification)

## System 3: Physics & Elections

### **Distinction** (Election 1)
The ability to differentiate one thing from another. The first fundamental operation: A is not B. From this simple binary choice, all complexity emerges.
- **Used in:** System 3 (Election 1)
- **Example:** Photon field: two states of oscillation distinguish themselves from background
- **Related terms:** [Binary Logic](#binary-logic), [Photon Field](#photon-field)

### **Movement** (Election 2)
The second operation: Position changes over time. Something that was here is now there. The combination of distinction + movement creates causality.
- **Used in:** System 3 (Election 2)
- **Example:** Vector field visualization: particles move according to forces; patterns emerge
- **Related terms:** [Distinction](#distinction), [Vector Field](#vector-field)

### **Spiral** (Election 3)
The pattern that emerges when multiple forces operate simultaneously with different phases. Spirals appear at every scale: DNA, galaxies, economics.
- **Used in:** System 3 (Election 3)
- **Example:** Compound motion: rotation + expansion = spiral pattern
- **Related terms:** [Movement](#movement), [Pattern](#pattern)

### **Direction** (Election 4)
Asymmetry: the field isn't uniform; some directions have more force than others. Creates persistent patterns and structures.
- **Used in:** System 3 (Election 4)
- **Example:** Directional field: arrows show both magnitude and direction; not a uniform cloud
- **Related terms:** [Spiral](#spiral), [Asymmetry](#asymmetry)

### **Time** (Election 5)
The integration of all previous elections through causality: what was → what is → what will be. Time emerges from the combination of distinction, movement, spirals, and direction.
- **Used in:** System 3 (Election 5)
- **Example:** Timeline shows causal chain: this state led to this next state, which led to current state
- **Related terms:** [Temporal Continuity](#temporal-continuity), [Causality](#causality)

### **Photon Field**
The unified model: photons are the fundamental carriers of change. All interactions are photon-mediated. The field contains all possible distinctions, movements, spirals, and directions.
- **Used in:** System 3 (foundation)
- **Example:** Light: photons carry energy across space, enabling distinction and change
- **Related terms:** [Distinction](#distinction), [Binary Foundation](#binary-foundation)

### **Vector Field**
A mathematical representation where each point in space has both magnitude and direction (a vector). Used to visualize how forces vary across space.
- **Used in:** System 3 (visualization)
- **Example:** Wind speed and direction at each location on a map
- **Related terms:** [Movement](#movement), [Direction](#direction)

### **Election**
One of five progressive demonstrations of how complexity emerges from simple operations: Distinction → Movement → Spirals → Direction → Time. Each builds on previous.
- **Used in:** System 3 (central framework)
- **Example:** Election 1 shows you can make one distinction; by Election 5, you see causal chains through time
- **Related terms:** [Distinction](#distinction), [Integration](#integration)

### **Pattern**
A repeated structure. Patterns appear because the same underlying forces operate repeatedly. Once a pattern is recognized, it becomes predictable.
- **Used in:** System 3 (observation)
- **Example:** Spiral appears in: DNA, galaxies, hurricanes, economics → same underlying forces at different scales
- **Related terms:** [Pattern Recognition](#pattern-recognition), [Spiral](#spiral)

### **Cosmic Unfolding**
The progression from quantum distinctions through increasingly complex structures at every scale up to civilization. Same structure everywhere because same forces operate everywhere.
- **Used in:** System 3 (integration)
- **Example:** Physics → chemistry → biology → psychology → sociology → civilization all follow same patterns
- **Related terms:** [Pattern](#pattern), [Integration](#integration)

## Cross-System Terms

### **Binary Foundation**
The principle underlying all three systems: Two states (0/1, yes/no, being/not-being) branch into all possible complexity. All three systems teach this through different domains.
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3 (integrated)
- **Example:** Gate (pass/not pass) → Binary logic (true/false) → Distinction (A/not-A)
- **Related terms:** [Binary Logic](#binary-logic), [Distinction](#distinction), [Gate](#gate)

### **Coherence**
The state where all parts align, reinforce, and make sense together. Incoherent systems fragment and fail. Coherent systems are stable and functional.
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3
- **Example:** Internal coherence (System 1), logical coherence (System 2), structural coherence (System 3)
- **Related terms:** [Internal Coherence](#internal-coherence), [Integration](#integration)

### **Structure**
The underlying pattern or framework that determines how things work. All three systems reveal structure: gates reveal structure of development, binary logic reveals structure of computation, physics reveals structure of universe.
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3 (throughout)
- **Example:** The structure of development prevents skipping gates; the structure of logic prevents hidden paths; the structure of physics determines causality
- **Related terms:** [Pattern](#pattern), [Gate](#gate), [Binary Logic](#binary-logic)

### **Verification Methodology**
The universal approach: Think the logic through completely, check for completeness/coherence, only then act. Applies to development (does this person understand Gate 3?), code (is this logic complete?), and physics (does this model predict observations?).
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3 (integrated)
- **Example:** Same verification approach: "Have I thought through all cases?" for a decision, a function, or a physical law
- **Related terms:** [Verification](#verification), [Completeness](#completeness)

### **Learning Capacity**
The ability to encounter new information, integrate it, and adjust future behavior accordingly. Present when someone has passed through key gates (especially Self-Correction/Gate 9). Absent in help-dependent systems.
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3
- **Example:** Person who learns from mistakes vs. person who repeats them
- **Related terms:** [Self-Correction](#self-correction), [Integration](#integration)

### **Leverage Point**
A place where a small change produces outsized effects. In System 1: a single help system decision cascades through all 10 gates. In System 2: one assumption error breaks everything. In System 3: small force differences create large structural differences.
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3
- **Example:** A parent's decision to "prevent all difficulty" is a leverage point affecting child's entire development
- **Related terms:** [Gate](#gate), [Impact](#impact)

### **Causality**
The principle that effects follow from causes. All three systems are built on this: gates enable competence (cause → effect), logic enables correct code (cause → effect), physics describes forces that produce motion (cause → effect).
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3
- **Example:** System 1: Passing gates → genuine competence. System 2: Verified logic → correct code. System 3: Forces → motion
- **Related terms:** [Causality Understanding](#causality-understanding), [Consequence Management](#consequence-management)

### **System**
A set of interrelated elements working together. All three wiki systems demonstrate this: Development system (10 gates), Computing system (verification tools), Physics system (elections).
- **Used in:** Systems 1, 2, 3 (throughout)
- **Example:** No single gate creates development; all 10 together do. No single tool ensures zero-error; all together do.
- **Related terms:** [Integration](#integration), [Structure](#structure)
