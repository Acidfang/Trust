# Gates in Coaching & Mentoring

## The Core Challenge

Effective mentoring requires passing gates. Most mentoring prevents gate-passage by solving problems instead of teaching problem-solving, creating dependency, and managing rather than developing.

---

## Gate 1: Agency → Support Your Mentee's Choices

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee making decisions about their path.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee: "Here's what I'm thinking"
- Mentor: "What do you think? What will you choose?"
- Mentee chooses, experiences results
- Mentee learns: my choices matter

**The Problem:** Mentor decides for mentee
- "Do exactly this"
- Mentee is passive
- No ownership of path

---

## Gate 2: Responsibility → Mentee Owns Their Results

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee understanding their results come from their effort.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee attempts something
- Results are mixed
- Mentor: "What did you learn? What caused this?"
- Mentee owns both success and failure
- Mentee learns: I'm responsible

**The Problem:** Mentor rescues mentee
- Results go bad; mentor fixes it
- Mentee never owns outcomes
- Stays dependent

---

## Gate 3: Complexity Navigation → Support Productive Struggle

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee working through hard problems.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee: "I don't know how to do this"
- Mentor: "This is hard. What have you tried?"
- "What might you try next?"
- Mentor doesn't solve; supports figuring out
- Mentee learns: I can work through hard things

**The Problem:** Mentor immediately solves
- Mentee never develops capability
- Dependent on mentor forever
- Mentee stays stuck

---

## Gate 4: Pattern Recognition → Point Out Patterns

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee seeing recurring structures in their own behavior.

**In Practice:**
- Mentor: "I notice you do X in situations like this"
- "Notice: this pattern appears here and here too"
- Mentee: "Ah! I do do that"
- Mentee learns to see own patterns
- Can change them intentionally

**The Problem:** Mentor doesn't name patterns
- Mentee keeps repeating same behavior
- Never sees the structure
- No growth

---

## Gate 5: Consequence Management → Help Mentee Process Failure

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee handling setbacks without quitting.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee fails at something
- Mentor: "This is real; you can handle it"
- "What did you learn? What's next?"
- Mentee processes failure; learns
- Mentee develops resilience

**The Problem:** Rescue mentee from failure
- Failure is prevented/fixed
- Mentee never learns resilience
- Fragile confidence

---

## Gate 6: Source Verification → Teach Mentee to Question

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee learning critical thinking.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee: "Should I do X?"
- Mentor: "What do YOU think? What evidence?"
- "Who says that? What's their expertise?"
- Mentee learns to evaluate sources
- Develops independent judgment

**The Problem:** Mentor is the authority
- Mentee accepts whatever mentor says
- Never develops own judgment
- Stays dependent on mentor

---

## Gate 7: Temporal Continuity → Help Mentee See Their Arc

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee recognizing their own progress.

**In Practice:**
- Mentor: "Remember when you couldn't do X?"
- "Look at you now"
- Mentee sees: progress despite feeling stuck
- Motivation is internal

**The Problem:** Mentee doesn't see progress
- Feels like no growth
- Gets discouraged
- Quits

---

## Gate 8: Causality Understanding → Help Mentee Diagnose Problems

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee understanding why things happen.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee: "I can't seem to get this right"
- Mentor: "Let's trace what's happening"
- "What's the root cause?"
- Mentee learns to diagnose
- Can solve own problems

**The Problem:** Mentor tells what's wrong
- Mentee never learns to diagnose
- Always needs mentor
- Stays dependent

---

## Gate 9: Self-Correction → Welcome Mentee's Feedback

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee feeling safe to correct mentor.

**In Practice:**
- Mentee: "I think that approach won't work"
- Mentor: "Tell me more; what am I missing?"
- Mentor considers feedback seriously
- Relationship is mutual, not hierarchical

**The Problem:** Mentor is always right
- Mentee stops providing feedback
- Mentor misses important input
- Both miss opportunities

---

## Gate 10: Integration → Help Mentee Hold Complexity

**What You're Enabling:** Mentee understanding nuance.

**In Practice:**
- Mentor: "This strength has this shadow side"
- "Both-and thinking is more mature than either-or"
- "This approach works AND has these limitations"
- Mentee develops wisdom

**The Problem:** Oversimplified thinking
- "Do this; never do that"
- Mentee can't navigate complexity
- Breaks when reality is more nuanced

---

## Effective Mentoring Framework

### Phase 1: Establish Relationship (Gates 1-2)
- Build trust
- Mentee chooses to be mentored
- Make responsibility clear

### Phase 2: Build Capability (Gates 3-5)
- Support productive struggle
- Name patterns mentee repeats
- Help process failure

### Phase 3: Develop Independence (Gates 6-8)
- Teach critical thinking
- Help diagnose problems
- Support self-sufficiency

### Phase 4: Mature Partnership (Gates 9-10)
- Welcome mentee's feedback
- Hold complexity together
- Mentee becomes capable mentor to others

---

## Mentor Role Definition

**Not your job:** Fix mentee's problems
**Your job:** Help mentee fix own problems

**Not your job:** Make mentee successful
**Your job:** Support mentee's development

**Not your job:** Know all answers
**Your job:** Ask powerful questions

**Not your job:** Keep mentee dependent
**Your job:** Develop mentee's independence

---

## Red Flags: Mentoring That Blocks Gates

❌ Mentor solves all problems
❌ Mentee never attempts anything risky
❌ Mentee can't make decisions without mentor
❌ Mentee doesn't see own patterns
❌ Feedback only flows one direction
❌ Mentee is always struggling
❌ No progress is visible

✅ Mentor asks questions
✅ Mentee attempts challenging things
✅ Mentee makes decisions and experiences results
✅ Mentee recognizes own patterns
✅ Feedback is mutual
✅ Mentee is building capability
✅ Progress is visible and growing

---

## Graduation Indicator

Mentoring is successful when:
- Mentee no longer needs mentor
- Mentee can solve own problems
- Mentee can teach others
- Mentee has developed their own voice
- Relationship shifts to peer/friend level
- Mentee becomes mentor to others
