# Gate 1 Discovery Template: Cause & Effect

## For Yourself

### Experiment: The Garden Test

A plant's health is completely dependent on your care. This makes cause-effect visible.

**What to do:**
1. Get a plant (easy one: basil, spider plant, or houseplant)
2. Nothing magical: just real responsibility
3. Set a calendar reminder: Water this plant every 3 days (exact day, exact time)
4. For 12 weeks, track:
   - Did you water it on schedule? (Yes/No)
   - What's the plant's state? (Thriving/OK/Struggling/Dead)
   - Forced observation: Is there a clear link between your action and outcome?

**What you'll discover:**
- Week 1-2: Plant looks same regardless (false belief: your watering doesn't matter yet)
- Week 3-4: Plant noticeably needs water when you skip it (causality visible)
- Week 5-8: Plant health perfectly mirrors your consistency (causality undeniable)
- Week 9-12: You understand: "My actions directly produce this outcome"

**The gate passes when:** You stop attributing plant health to luck/seasons/plant's nature and recognize: "This plant's state IS the direct result of what I did or didn't do."

### Variation: Decision Tracking

Pick ONE decision where you control the outcome:

- Writing a project proposal by deadline
- Budget management 
- Fitness consistency (workout 3x/week for 8 weeks)
- Learning a skill (30 min daily for 12 weeks)

**Track:**
- Did I do it? When?
- What was the result?
- Could I have predicted this result from my input?

**Observation:** After 12 weeks, do you see clear cause-effect, or does it still feel random?

## For Children (Ages 6-12)

### Setup: Real Responsibility

Give the child **one plant they entirely own**:
- They water it
- They decide when
- You observe but don't intervene (until plant is dying, then educational conversation)
- Natural consequence: Plant thrives or dies based on their care

### The Learning Arc

**Week 1-2:** Child waters sporadically. Plant starts declining. Child says: "Plants are hard" or "This plant is bad."

**Your job:** Not to rescue the plant. Point to evidence: "What happened on days you didn't water it?"

**Week 3-4:** Plant noticeably wilts when not watered. Child begins understanding: "Oh, it needs water."

**Week 5+:** Child develops reliable watering habit because they see the cause-effect directly.

**Gate 1 passes when:** Child says something like "If I don't water it, it dies. If I do, it gets better." No longer external (plant's fault), now causal (their action → result).

### Variations by Age

**Age 6-8:** Plant in their room. They fully own watering schedule.

**Age 9-11:** Plant + one other simple responsibility (feeding pet, maintaining area of house).

**Age 12+:** Multiple responsibilities with interdependent outcomes (if they don't feed pet, pet suffers; if they don't do laundry, they run out of clothes).

## For Organizations

### Minimum Viable Gate 1 Discovery

Pick the lowest-stakes project:
- Small team
- Real deliverable
- 4-week timeline
- Full transparency on cause-effect

**Week 1:**
- Team makes plan
- Explicit: "These actions will produce these results"

**Week 2-3:**
- Team executes
- Track: Did they do what they said? What resulted?

**Week 4:**
- Retrospective: Causality check
- "Our actions produced these outcomes—here's exactly how"

**The gate passes when:** Team stops saying "That was bad luck" and starts saying "We caused that result by doing/not doing X."

## Troubleshooting

**"The plant died and they're still not seeing causality"**
→ The gate may not be passing yet. That's OK. The plant dying is actually *teaching* the gate. The lesson is: "I can cause outcome I don't want." That's still gate-passage.

**"They're making excuses"** ("Someone else moved it," "It needs special soil")
→ Gently: "OK, that might be true. Let's control for that next time and see."
→ The gate isn't fully passed until they own the result completely, including when it fails.

**"I keep rescuing them because I can't stand watching them fail"**
→ That's the help system trap. Rescuing prevents the gate from passing.
→ The discomfort you feel? That's you recognizing: "If I rescue them, they never discover causality."
→ The compassionate thing is to let them experience consequence fully.

## When Gate 1 Is Fully Passed

- Successes: "I did that"
- Failures: "I caused that"
- Outcomes: "Directly linked to my actions"
- No victim mentality; no magical thinking
- Can move to Gate 2 (responsibility attribution)
