## CORE COMMUNICATION ISSUES

### Issue 1: Equivocation Fallacy (Same Word, Different Meanings)

**"AI" in this conversation means different things:**

**Screenshot evidence: Image 5**

Jennifer: "Why are you having AI write your comments? Can't you think through how to write these ideas yourself?"

Jennifer's frame here: "AI-assistance" = "letting AI think for you"

Joe (Image 5): "The thinking is mine. I use tools to structure it so it's clear and testable."

Joe's frame: "AI-assistance" = "tool for formatting pre-existing thoughts"

**The core equivocation:**
- Both are using "AI" to mean completely different things
- Jennifer: Assuming the tool generates the thought
- Joe: Assuming the tool only structures existing thought
- Neither questions if their definition is shared

**Why this matters:**
Jennifer's actual evidence ("it looks AI-generated") doesn't prove her claim ("you're using AI to think")
Joe's actual defense ("the thinking is mine") doesn't address her claim ("the output looks machine-made")

**What needed to happen:**
"When you say AI is thinking for me, do you mean: [A] generating novel thoughts, [B] structuring existing thoughts, or [C] something else?"

**What actually happened:**
Both sides assume the other understands their definition. They don't.
